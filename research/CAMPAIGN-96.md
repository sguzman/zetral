# Campaign XCVI — Hilbertization of Fréchet spectral models

Date opened: 2026-11-27. Status: ACTIVE.

## Precise question

Can a spectral interpretation of the zeta zeros on a nuclear Fréchet space be
converted into a positive Hilbert-space Hilbert–Pólya model while preserving
its exact zero spectrum and algebraic multiplicities? The first test is
whether Meyer's original nuclear Fréchet topology can be generated by a
Hilbert norm; if not, determine what is and is not implied for weaker Hilbert
completions.

## Literature and initial evidence

Primary source: R. Meyer, *A spectral interpretation for the zeros of the
Riemann zeta function*, [arXiv:math/0412277](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0412277),
especially the introduction and §4. The paper states that it constructs a
nuclear Fréchet space `H^0_-` with a continuous generator `D_-` whose
spectrum is the nontrivial zero set with algebraic multiplicity; the
transpose action on the continuous dual is also described explicitly.
The spectral space is topological-vector-space based, not a positive Hilbert
space. The exact role of zero dependence in any subsequent Hilbert completion
is an open part of this campaign.

Source conventions checked against v3 (2013): §2 defines `H_cap` as the
**even** Schwartz functions with `f(0)=Ff(0)=0` (equation (11) and the line
following the Poisson formula); Theorem 3.3 states `Zf in H_-` iff
`f in H_cap`; equation (13) gives the Mellin multiplier identity initially
for `Re(s)>1` on `S_>`; and equation (14) plus the preceding analytic
continuation gives the corresponding entire identity for `f in H_cap`.
Using equation (13) alone on arbitrary Mellin lines would be unjustified.
Meyer §2 defines `H_-` as the intersection of the multiplicative Schwartz
spaces for all real weights, supplying the endpoint decay used in the
weighted Hilbert inclusions here.

## MHS-01 — Compatible Hilbert topology obstruction (complete)

### Question

Can the original topology of Meyer's `H^0_-` be induced by any Hilbert norm?

### Claim under audit

No, provided the stated nuclear Fréchet structure is infinite-dimensional.
The paper's own spectral statement gives infinitely many eigenvalues because
the nontrivial zero set is infinite, hence `H^0_-` is infinite-dimensional.
If its topology were Hilbertizable, it would be a nuclear Banach space. Its
identity map would then be nuclear, hence compact; compactness of the identity
forces the unit ball to be compact and the space finite-dimensional, a
contradiction.

This rules out only a topology-compatible Hilbertization of the original
space. It does not rule out a weaker Hilbert norm on a dense subspace, a
different completion, or a new self-adjoint operator with the same zeros.
Any such completion must be checked for closability, spectrum preservation,
and whether its norm imports zero locations or RH.

### Source-scope audit

Meyer defines
`H^0_- = H_- / Z H_cap ≅ H_union / Z H_+` and gives it the quotient
topology. His introduction states that `H^0_-` is nuclear Fréchet and that
the induced generator `D_-` has the nontrivial zeros as its spectrum with
analytic multiplicity. Corollary 4.2 separately identifies the eigenvalues
and multiplicities of the transpose on `(H^0_-)′`. These are related but
distinct spectral statements; the topology obstruction below is applied to
`H^0_-` itself, not silently transferred to its continuous dual.

### Proof

The completed zeta function has infinitely many nontrivial zeros. Since
Meyer's `D_-` has those values in its spectrum, `H^0_-` cannot be
finite-dimensional. Suppose its nuclear Fréchet topology were generated by a
Hilbert norm. It would then be a nuclear Banach space, so its identity map
would be nuclear and therefore compact. But a compact identity makes the
closed unit ball compact, which by Riesz's lemma is possible only in finite
dimension. Contradiction. Thus no Hilbert norm induces the original topology.

This is a direct consequence of Meyer's stated construction and standard
nuclear-space facts. It is not a no-go for a non-equivalent weaker Hilbert
topology or for completion of a different dense domain, and it does not show
that such a completion retains any zero eigenvalues. The argument is
hand-checked; no Lean proof or independent external audit was used.

## Backlog and experimental design

1. **MHS-01 complete:** the original quotient topology is not Hilbertizable.
2. **MHS-02.1 complete within the `L²(dx)` candidate:** the Mellin multiplier
   graph-core and dense-range arguments below show the quotient seminorm is
   zero. This is a scoped manual result, not an external/formal proof.
3. Test different weights/completions and
   falsify naive spectrum preservation; do not generalize a one-completion
   failure to all Hilbert spaces.
4. Compare with de Branges/Clark models already in the repository; do not
   count a zero-dependent model as an independent Hilbert–Pólya construction.

## MHS-02.1 — The natural additive `L²` quotient candidate collapses

Let `V:L²((0,infinity),dx)->L²(R,dy)` be the unitary
`(Vf)(y)=e^(y/2)f(e^y)`. Its Fourier transform is the Mellin transform of
`f` on the line `s=1/2+i tau`. For `f` in Meyer's
`H_cap={f in S(R): f is even, f(0)=Ff(0)=0}`, the source's
Poisson-summation theorem
gives `Zf in H_-`, and analytic continuation of its Mellin identity yields

    F V(Zf)(tau) = zeta(1/2+i tau) F V(f)(tau).

Here `H_-` embeds in `L²(dx)` because it has rapid decay at both endpoints.
Thus `Z|H_cap` is a restriction of the closed Fourier multiplier
`M_m`, `m(tau)=zeta(1/2+i tau)`.

The graph-core point is as follows. The multiplier symbol and all its
derivatives have polynomial growth on this fixed vertical line, by
Euler–Maclaurin bounds in a fixed strip and Cauchy estimates. Therefore
`C_c^infinity(R)` is a graph core for `M_m`: Schwartz functions are a core by
weighted `L²` approximation on the Fourier side, and spatial cutoffs converge
in Schwartz topology, which `M_m` preserves. Under `V`, the test functions
`C_c^infinity(R)` satisfying

    Lambda(g) := integral_R e^(y/2) g(y) dy = 0

come from compactly supported smooth even functions on the original line
with `f(0)=Ff(0)=0`, hence lie in `H_cap`. This constrained test space is
still graph-dense: for any compactly supported `g`, cancel `Lambda(g)` by a
translated bump `a_R psi(y-R)`, where `Lambda(psi) != 0` and
`a_R=O(e^(-R/2))`. Translation commutes with `M_m`, so both the Hilbert norm
and multiplier norm of the correction tend to zero. Consequently `H_cap` is
a graph core and the closure of `Z|H_cap` in this `L²(dx)` model is exactly
`M_m`.

The multiplier `m` is nonzero almost everywhere: the zeros of the analytic
function `zeta(1/2+i tau)` are discrete on the line. Hence `M_m` has dense
range. Since Meyer's quotient is `H_- / ZH_cap`, the seminorm inherited from
`L²(dx)` on that quotient is identically zero; its Hausdorff Hilbert
completion is the zero space. This rejects this particular natural
completion as a Hilbert–Pólya realization.

An independent orthogonal-complement check gives the same density conclusion.
If `q` is orthogonal to `Z H_cap`, graph-core approximation makes it
orthogonal to `M_m(D(M_m))`, so
`q in ker(M_m*)=ker(M_conj(m))`. Because `m!=0` almost everywhere, `q=0`.
This independently verifies the dense-range step and the zero quotient
seminorm.

The derivation is a scoped manual result using the stated vertical-strip
derivative bounds; the graph-core and dense-range steps were rechecked
separately in two ways. It does not rule out a
different Hilbert weight, topology, or domain, and it does not transfer the
Fréchet spectrum to any Hilbert space. No computation, Lean proof,
second-person audit, or novelty claim is made. The next investigation tests
whether another natural completion avoids dense range without importing the
zeros.

## MHS-02.2 — Collapse for a family of weighted multiplicative `L²` norms

The same argument works for

    H_sigma = L²((0,infinity), x^(2 sigma) dx/x),   sigma > -2, sigma != 1.

Every `H_-` function lies in each `H_sigma`. If `f∈H_cap`, evenness and
`f(0)=0` imply `f(x)=O(x²)` at the origin, while Schwartz decay controls
infinity; therefore `f∈H_sigma` for `sigma>-2`. Also `Zf∈H_-⊂H_sigma`.
The unitary map
`V_sigma f(y)=e^(sigma y)f(e^y)` sends the Mellin transform on
`Re(s)=sigma` to Fourier transform. On `H_cap`, analytic continuation of the
Meyer identity gives

    F V_sigma(Zf)(tau)
       = zeta(sigma+i tau) F V_sigma(f)(tau).

For fixed `sigma != 1`, the symbol `m_sigma(tau)=zeta(sigma+i tau)` and all
derivatives have polynomial growth: choose a fixed vertical strip around the
line avoiding the pole at 1, use Euler–Maclaurin continuation for a
polynomial bound, then Cauchy estimates. The Fourier multiplier
`M_(m_sigma)` therefore has `C_c^infinity(R)` as a graph core.

The defining moment condition `Ff(0)=0` becomes

    Lambda_sigma(g) = integral_R e^((1-sigma)y) g(y) dy = 0,

under `V_sigma`; `f(0)=0` is automatic for the compactly supported tests
under consideration. For `sigma<1`, translate the moment-correcting bump to
`+infinity`; for `sigma>1`, translate it to `-infinity`. Its correcting
amplitude decays exponentially, while translation preserves the multiplier
graph norm up to that amplitude. Hence the constrained compact tests from
`H_cap` remain a graph core for every such `sigma`.

The line function `zeta(sigma+i tau)` is analytic and not identically zero,
so its zeros are discrete and the multiplication operator has dense range.
The range `Z H_cap` is graph-core dense in that range and is therefore dense
in every `H_sigma` above. Consequently the quotient seminorm inherited from
any of these weighted Hilbert spaces is identically zero. This includes the
Haar norm (`sigma=0`) and the additive `dx` norm (`sigma=1/2`), among others.

The excluded line `sigma=1` passes through the zeta pole; there the multiplier
has a singularity at `tau=0` and the moment-zero domain interacts with it.
The graph-core correction above does not apply, so this line remains an
explicit next test. This is still only a family of inherited quotient norms,
not a general obstruction to Hilbert completions.

## MHS-02.3 — The pole line `sigma=1` also has dense range

The excluded line can be handled by separating the pole. Under `V_1`, the
Mellin multiplier is

    m_1(tau) = zeta(1+i tau) = 1/(i tau) + b(tau),

where `b` extends smoothly across `tau=0` and has polynomially bounded
derivatives. Meyer's condition `Ff(0)=0` becomes `integral g(y)dy=0`, which
is exactly the vanishing needed for `m_1(tau) Fg(tau)` to be square
integrable at the pole.

The graph domain of `M_(m_1)` has norm equivalent near zero to
`||g||_2 + || Fg/tau ||_2`, and away from zero to a polynomially weighted
Fourier norm. Its Fourier-side domain is dense: smooth compactly supported
functions whose support avoids `tau=0` are dense in the weighted `L²` space.
Their inverse transforms are Schwartz functions of mean zero.

These Schwartz zero-mean functions can be approximated in graph norm by
compactly supported zero-mean functions. Let `h` be Schwartz with
`integral h=0`, let `p_R=chi_R h` for standard expanding cutoffs, and write
`delta_R=integral p_R -> 0`. Choose a fixed compact bump `psi` with
`integral psi=1`, set `psi_L(y)=L^(-1)psi(y/L)`, and put
`q_R=p_R-delta_R psi_L`. Then `q_R` is compactly supported with zero mean.
For `r_R=h-p_R`, one has `integral r_R=-delta_R` and
`|F r_R(tau)+delta_R| <= epsilon_R |tau|`, where
`epsilon_R=integral |y r_R(y)|dy -> 0`. Also

    || delta_R (F psi(L tau)-1)/tau ||_2
       = C |delta_R| sqrt(L),

with finite `C`, since `(F psi(u)-1)/u` is bounded near zero and decays as
`1/u` at infinity. Choosing `L=max(1,|delta_R|^(-1))` makes this tend to
zero; the remaining low-frequency contribution is `O(epsilon_R)` and the
high-frequency contribution tends to zero by Schwartz convergence. The
regular multiplier part `b` also converges because it has polynomial growth
and `r_R, delta_R psi_L` tend to zero in the corresponding Sobolev norms.
Thus `C_c^infinity` zero-mean tests are a graph core for `M_(m_1)`.

Under `V_1`, these tests correspond to compactly supported smooth even
functions with `f(0)=Ff(0)=0`, so they lie in `H_cap`. The source identity
therefore identifies the closure of `Z|H_cap` with `M_(m_1)`. Its range is
dense because the multiplier is nonzero almost everywhere; the pole at the
single point `tau=0` does not affect this. The quotient Hilbert seminorm
inherited from `H_1=L²(x dx)` consequently also collapses.

This is a manual graph-core derivation, not formally checked. The weighted
Sobolev estimate for the regular part and the approximation argument should
receive a separate audit before external use. It extends the collapse result
to the previously exceptional pole weight; it remains a statement only about
this induced family of Hilbert quotient norms.

## MHS-02.4 — Log-Sobolev completions also have zero quotient (manual audit)

For fixed `sigma` and integer `k>=0`, put

    H_(sigma,k) = {f : V_sigma f in H^k(R)},
    ||f||_(sigma,k) = ||V_sigma f||_(H^k),

where `V_sigma f(y)=e^(sigma y)f(e^y)`. This is a Hilbert norm on a dense
completion of the multiplicative test space, with `H_(sigma,0)=H_sigma`.
Every Meyer `H_-` function belongs to all these spaces because it is rapidly
decreasing with all multiplicative derivatives at both endpoints. The
compact log-coordinate tests used above belong to `H_cap`, and their zeta
images belong to `H_(sigma,k)` because their Fourier transforms are Schwartz
and `zeta(sigma+i tau)` has polynomial growth.

For `sigma!=1`, let `T_sigma g=F^{-1}(m_sigma Fg)` on compactly supported
`g` satisfying `Lambda_sigma(g)=integral e^((1-sigma)y)g(y)dy=0`. Suppose
`h in H^k` is orthogonal to all such `T_sigma g` in the `H^k` inner product.
The functional

    S(g) = <h,T_sigma g>_(H^k)

extends to a tempered distribution: in Fourier coordinates its pairing is
bounded by `||h||_(H^k)` times a Schwartz seminorm of `g`, since `m_sigma`
has polynomial growth. It annihilates the kernel of the nonzero functional
`Lambda_sigma` on `C_c^infinity`, so `S=c Lambda_sigma` for a scalar `c`.
Writing `a=1-sigma`, the latter is the distribution `c e^(a y)`. If
`sigma!=1`, this exponential is not tempered unless `c=0`; hence `S=0`.
On every open interval where `m_sigma` is nonzero, the Fourier identity for
`S=0` implies `Fh=0`. The zeros of the nonzero analytic function
`zeta(sigma+i tau)` are discrete, so `h=0` in `H^k`. Thus the range is dense
in `H_(sigma,k)` for every fixed non-pole `sigma` and finite integer `k`.

For `sigma=1`, the same orthogonal-complement argument applies after using
the mean-zero graph core from MHS-02.3 in the stronger norm

    ||g||_(H^k) + ||m_1 Fg||_(H^k).

The cutoff tail `r_R` tends to zero in every fixed Sobolev norm. The broad
correction `delta_R psi_L`, with `L=|delta_R|^(-1)` when `delta_R!=0`, tends
to zero in each such norm by scaling. Near `tau=0`, the pole term is
controlled by
`|F r_R(tau)+delta_R| <= epsilon_R |tau|` and
`||delta_R(F psi(L tau)-1)/tau||_2=C|delta_R|sqrt(L)`; the extra Sobolev
weight is bounded there. For `|tau|>=1`, the cutoff tail tends to zero in
every fixed polynomially weighted Fourier norm. For the broad correction,
scaling gives `||delta_R (1+|tau|)^N Fpsi(L tau)||_2=
O(|delta_R|/sqrt(L))` for every fixed `N` and `L>=1`; on `|tau|>=1`, the
additional factor `1/tau` is harmless and the Schwartz tail of `Fpsi` only
improves the bound. This controls both the pole term and the
polynomial-growth regular part `b`. This proves the needed graph-core
approximation for each fixed finite `k`. Orthogonality then extends to
Fourier tests supported away from zero, forcing `Fh=0` off the singleton
`{0}`; an `L²` function supported on one point is zero. Hence the pole-line
range is dense as well.

Consequently the quotient seminorm inherited from every `H_(sigma,k)` is
zero for finite integer `k>=0` and real `sigma`; this includes the pole line.
This is a wider negative result for the simple Sobolev-strengthened Mellin
norms, not for arbitrary Hilbert norms or completions. The non-pole
orthogonal-complement argument and pole-line scaling estimates were rederived
in this audit batch; this remains a hand-checked derivation, not a formal
proof or an independent external review. No novelty claim is made.

## MHS-02.5 — Polynomially moderate Mellin densities (generalization)

The same obstruction extends beyond the Sobolev weights. Let `w` be a
measurable function satisfying, almost everywhere, for some `N>=0` and
constants `0<c<=C<infinity`,

    c(1+tau^2)^(-N) <= w(tau) <= C(1+tau^2)^N.

Use the Hilbert norm

    ||f||_(sigma,w)^2 = integral_R w(tau) |F(V_sigma f)(tau)|^2 d tau.

The Schwartz class is dense in `L²(w d tau)` under these two-sided
polynomial bounds, and all `H_-` Mellin transforms belong to it. For
`sigma!=1`, if `h` is orthogonal to `Z H_cap`, then

    S(g)=integral_R w(tau) conjugate(Fh(tau))
                         zeta(sigma+i tau) Fg(tau) d tau

annihilates all compactly supported `g` with
`integral e^((1-sigma)y)g(y)dy=0`. The upper bound on `w`, the polynomial
growth of zeta on this non-pole line, and `h in L²(w)` make `S` a tempered
distribution. The annihilator of the kernel of that one moment functional
is its scalar span, represented by `e^((1-sigma)y)`. Since this is not
tempered for `sigma!=1`, `S=0`. Locally `w` is bounded above and below, so
the Fourier-side identity and nonvanishing of zeta almost everywhere imply
`h=0` almost everywhere. Thus the range is dense.

For `sigma=1`, choose an integer Sobolev order `k>=N`. The mean-zero
compact-test graph core proved in MHS-02.3–.4 converges in
`||g||_(H^k)+||m_1 Fg||_(H^k)`, which dominates the corresponding
`L²(w)` graph norm by the upper polynomial bound. Orthogonality therefore
extends to inverse Fourier transforms of smooth tests supported away from
`tau=0`. It forces `w conjugate(Fh)m_1=0` as a distribution off zero. Since
`w>0` almost everywhere and `m_1` is nonzero almost everywhere, `h=0`.
This proves dense range on the pole line too.

Therefore every Hilbert completion in this class—Mellin-Plancherel with a
strictly positive, polynomially moderate absolutely continuous density—has
zero quotient by `Z H_cap`. This includes MHS-02.1–.4. The result does not
cover singular spectral measures (including atomic masses at zeros), weights
with super-polynomial growth or decay, or Hilbert norms not diagonal in the
Mellin transform. It is a manually derived generalization; no formal,
independent external audit, or novelty determination has been completed.

## MHS-03.1 — Exact criterion for a singular diagonal Mellin model

This is an abstract classification inside the Mellin-diagonal ansatz, not a
construction of a new norm on Meyer's quotient. Let `mu` be a positive
locally finite Borel measure on `R`, let
`m_sigma(tau)=zeta(sigma+i tau)`, and let `M_m` be its maximal multiplication
operator on `L²(mu)`. For `sigma=1`, require `mu({0})=0` so that the zeta
pole is absent from the measure space; otherwise this ordinary multiplication
ansatz is undefined there and needs a separate singular-domain construction.
Under this condition its domain is dense and the operator is closed. The
adjoint is multiplication by `conjugate(m_sigma)`, so

    closure(range M_m)^perp = ker(M_m*)
                            = L²(mu restricted to Z_sigma),

where `Z_sigma={tau : m_sigma(tau)=0}`, with the pole excluded when
`sigma=1`.
Thus the Hilbert quotient by the closed multiplier range is nonzero exactly
when `mu(Z_sigma)>0`. Since `Z_sigma` is discrete, this requires at least one
atom at a zeta zero on that line. This is the precise contrast with the
absolutely continuous measures of MHS-02.5.

For the normalized dilation group on `L²(mu)`, the generator is (up to sign)
the self-adjoint multiplication operator `A=M_tau`, with domain
`{g: tau*g in L²(mu)}`. Its spectrum is `supp(mu)` and its eigenspace at
`gamma` is `L²(mu|_{ {gamma} })`; in scalar `L²(mu)`, an atom contributes
one-dimensional eigenspace, regardless of its mass. Therefore an exact
spectral model of all zeta-zero ordinates within this ansatz requires:

1. support of `mu` exactly at the ordinates, to avoid extra continuous
   spectrum;
2. atoms at each ordinate, for point spectrum and nonzero quotient;
3. vector-valued fibers of dimension equal to analytic zero multiplicity if
   multiplicities are to match when a multiple zero occurs.

This calculation classifies the **maximal** multiplier model. It does not
prove that Meyer's restricted `Z(H_cap)` is a graph core in `L²(mu)` for a
singular measure, nor that its quotient generator is intertwined with
`M_tau`. Those domain and intertwining steps are essential and remain open;
the support criterion alone is not a Hilbert–Pólya construction.

Choosing `mu` directly as a sum of atoms at the zero ordinates (and choosing
the fibers from their multiplicities) simply imports the target data. That
is a zero-dependent encoding, not an independent Hilbert–Pólya construction.
A measure defined independently, for example from prime-side data, whose
atoms and support can then be proved to have these exact properties would be
substantive; no such measure is constructed here. The multiplication facts
above are elementary consequences of the spectral theorem for multiplication
operators. The RH-conditional zero-supported measure and its scalar
multiplicity limitation are already developed in
`research/operators/B3-09-nevanlinna-kernel-and-zero-measure.md` and
`research/operators/B5-07-clark-model-for-normalized-limit.md`; B4-01 records
the classical Lagarias/Hinkkanen positivity equivalence. MHS-03.1 is a
cross-campaign diagnostic and scope clarification, not a novelty claim.

## MHS-03.2 — A zero-dependent Hilbert completion of Meyer's quotient

Assume RH and let `Gamma` be the set of distinct signed ordinates of the
nontrivial zeros. Put

    mu = sum_(gamma in Gamma) exp(-gamma^2) delta_gamma,
    Jf(gamma) = fhat(1/2+i gamma),
    p_mu(f)^2 = sum_(gamma in Gamma) exp(-gamma^2)
                         |fhat(1/2+i gamma)|^2.

The Riemann–von Mangoldt bound implies `mu(R)<infinity`. For `f in H_-`,
its Mellin transform on the critical line is rapidly decreasing, so `p_mu`
is finite and continuous on the Meyer Fréchet space. If `g in H_cap`, the
continued Mellin identity gives
`(Zg)hat(1/2+i gamma)=zeta(1/2+i gamma) ghat(1/2+i gamma)=0`.
Consequently `p_mu` descends to a seminorm on `H^0_-`.

The image of `H_cap` under `J` is dense in `L²(mu)`. To prove this, it
suffices to test compact log-coordinate functions `g in C_c^infinity(R)`
satisfying the one transformed Fourier-moment condition
`integral e^(y/2)g(y)dy=0`; their inverse Mellin transforms extend to even
compactly supported smooth functions in `H_cap`. Suppose
`c=(c_gamma) in L²(mu)` is orthogonal to all their evaluation vectors. The
finite complex measure

    nu = sum_(gamma in Gamma) exp(-gamma^2) conjugate(c_gamma) delta_gamma

is finite by Cauchy–Schwarz and `mu(R)<infinity`. Its Fourier transform
`F(y)=integral exp(i gamma y)d nu(gamma)` is bounded and continuous.
Orthogonality says `integral g(y)F(y)dy=0` whenever
`integral e^(y/2)g(y)dy=0`. The annihilator of this codimension-one test
kernel consists of scalar multiples of `e^(y/2)` as distributions. Since
`F` is bounded and `e^(y/2)` is unbounded, the scalar is zero. Uniqueness
of the Fourier transform of finite measures gives `nu=0`, hence `c=0`.
Therefore `J(H_cap)` is dense, and so is `J(H_-)`.

The Hausdorff completion of `H^0_-` in the seminorm `p_mu` is thus
`L²(mu)`. Meyer's dilation `lambda_(e^t) f(x)=f(e^(-t)x)` satisfies

    J(e^(-t/2) lambda_(e^t) f)(gamma)
        = exp(i gamma t) Jf(gamma).

The normalized dilation extends to a strongly continuous unitary group;
Stone's theorem identifies its self-adjoint generator with multiplication by
`gamma` on the maximal domain `{c: sum exp(-gamma^2) gamma^2
|c_gamma|^2 < infinity}`. Since the ordinates have no finite accumulation,
the spectrum is exactly the set of distinct ordinates, with one-dimensional
eigenspace at each. This recovers the zero set as a self-adjoint spectrum
under RH, and explicitly handles the graph-core/density and intertwining
steps missing from the abstract MHS-03.1 criterion. The seminorm kernel is
invariant under dilation because the action multiplies each evaluation by a
nonzero scalar, so the unitary group is genuinely induced from the Meyer
quotient before completion.

This does **not** give an independent Hilbert–Pólya construction: RH is
assumed to put every zero on the real ordinate axis, and the atoms are
defined from those zero ordinates. The scalar model also records analytic
multiplicity only as optional atom weights, not eigenspace dimension; if any
zero is multiple, exact self-adjoint multiplicity is not matched by this
scalar completion. No simplicity assumption is made. The norm further
collapses every class invisible to zero evaluation, so it is a Hausdorff
quotient of Meyer's Fréchet quotient, not an equivalent Hilbert topology.
This is a concrete conditional benchmark and a manual analytic deduction;
Meyer's Corollary 4.2 already identifies evaluation functionals and derivative
jets in the continuous dual, while repository notes B3-09/B5-07 already give
the zero-defined scalar spectral measure/model under RH. Thus no novelty is
claimed; the specific added link is density of the value-evaluation map from
the compact source tests into the weighted atomic Hilbert space. A targeted
prior-art review, formal proof and external independent audit remain undone.

## Dependencies and information gain

Dependencies are Meyer's exact function-space definitions, standard nuclear
space facts, tempered-distribution facts, and the classical infinitude of
nontrivial zeta zeros. No large dataset or computation is needed. Expected
information gain: distinguish a
mere change of topology from an actual route to a self-adjoint operator, and
identify whether the existing Fréchet construction has a meaningful Hilbert
completion.

## Acceptance and verification

Accept MHS-01 when each hypothesis is sourced and the topological argument is
checked independently. For any proposed completion, acceptance requires a
specified dense domain, closed/self-adjoint generator, exact spectrum and
multiplicities, and no extra spectrum. A weaker completion that changes the
spectrum is a negative result for that completion, not a no-go for all
Hilbert–Pólya constructions. No RH proof or novelty claim is an acceptance
criterion.
