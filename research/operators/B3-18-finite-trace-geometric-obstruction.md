# B3-18 — The function-field Frobenius pairing needs a non-finite trace analogue

Date: 2026-11-27. Status: exact obstruction to a direct finite-trace
transplant; semifinite and regularized constructions remain open.

## Question

Can Suzuki's Rosati/Frobenius trace pairing for a curve be copied to the
Riemann zeta function using a finite tracial algebra whose unitary spectrum
is the set of zero ordinates modulo a fixed period?

## Function-field construction

For a curve over `F_q`, the source uses the finite-dimensional algebra
generated by normalized Frobenius `F=q^(-1/2) pi`. Its trace satisfies
`tau(F^n)=p_n` and `tau(1)=2g`; the Weil form is

    Q_C(u,v) = h integral_0^h tau(X_u(t) X_v(t)^*) dt.

The resulting global Hilbert space is `L^2((0,h), E)`, where `E` is
finite-dimensional, and translation by `h` is unitarily equivalent to
multiplication by `F`. These are exact source theorems (Suzuki,
arXiv:2610.41115v1, §§8–9).

## Finite-trace obstruction for zeta

Suppose a unitary `F` and a finite positive trace `tau` were to encode the
zeta zero measure in the direct spectral way

    tau(P(F) P(F)^*) = sum_gamma m_gamma |P(e^(i h gamma))|^2

for Laurent polynomials `P`, with fixed `h>0`. Taking `P=1` forces

    tau(1) = sum_gamma m_gamma.

The right-hand side is infinite: the Riemann–von Mangoldt formula gives
`sum_{0<gamma<=T} m_gamma ~ (T/(2 pi)) log T`. This contradicts finiteness
of `tau(1)`. Thus the literal finite-trace version of the function-field
Rosati model cannot represent the full zeta zero measure. Equivalently,
the pointwise moments `sum_gamma m_gamma e^(i n h gamma)` cannot be finite
trace moments for the unitary powers `F^n`.

## Falsification and scope

- The contradiction uses the constant polynomial only, so it is independent
  of RH, zero locations, or any numerical computation.
- It does not prohibit a semifinite trace, a regularized/distributional
  trace, a nonunital ideal on which the test-function elements are trace
  class, or an explicit-formula functional that is not a trace.
- For smooth compactly supported test functions, rapid decay of their Fourier
  transforms can make the zero-side quadratic sum finite even though the
  total zero counting measure is infinite. The obstruction concerns the
  unweighted unit and polynomial moments, not the Weil form on its test
  domain.
- No arithmetic construction of a semifinite Frobenius-like object is
  supplied. This is a necessary design constraint, not a no-go for a
  number-field geometric pairing.
- No novelty claim is made; the result is the direct finite-trace test
  applied to Suzuki's published construction and the classical zero count.

## Prior-art reconciliation: the global model already exists

Suzuki, *On the Hilbert space derived from the Weil distribution*,
Canadian Journal of Mathematics (published online 2025-11-03), defines the
Weil form on compactly supported smooth functions, proves it positive under
RH, and constructs its global completion `H_W`. Under RH, Theorem 1.1
identifies it with a de Branges/model space, and §6 transports the
self-adjoint multiplication extension to `H_W`; its eigenvalues are exactly
the distinct ordinates. The source explicitly states that each operator
eigenspace is one-dimensional, so analytic zero multiplicity is not
geometric multiplicity. The RH premise can be composed at theorem level
with the separately kernel-checked Lean result, but Zetral has not
independently audited Suzuki's full proof.

Thus the finite-trace test does not block a prime-side Hilbert–Pólya space:
the existing completion permits infinite total spectral mass. It leaves the
same key gap as the canonical-system route: a noncircular,
multiplicity-sensitive operator correspondence. The direct derivative-kernel
attempt in PWPB-03.19 collapses to rank one. Next seek independent arithmetic
feature families or a rigorously delimited obstruction to that repair; do
not repeat the already-published scalar global construction.
