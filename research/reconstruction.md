# R02 — Reconstruction of Suzuki's spectral convergence

Source: Masatoshi Suzuki, arXiv:2610.32704v1 (2026-10-23), https://arxiv.org/html/2610.32704v1, §§2–5; inspected 2026-11-24 Mexico. Antecedent: arXiv:2606.09096v4, https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09096v4. Status: human-readable reconstruction of published mathematics, not a novel result or machine-checked proof. RH is an explicit hypothesis throughout. The local coercivity, sampling isomorphism, and form-core facts are imported results, not re-proved here.

## 1. Conventions and spaces (§2.1–2.3)
Use F[u](z)=integral u(x) exp(izx) dx, inner products linear in their first argument, A(z)=xi(1/2-iz), with xi as in the source. Under RH the distinct ordinates Gamma are real, symmetric under negation, nonzero, with multiplicities m_gamma=m_-gamma. The weighted sum of 1/gamma^2 is finite.

The Weil inner product Q(u,v)=sum_gamma m_gamma F[u](gamma) conjugate(F[v](gamma)) is also given by the arithmetic explicit formula. For fixed a>0, let A_a denote the self-adjoint form operator in ordinary L2(-a,a). Its lower spectral bound lambda_a is strictly positive (imported local coercivity, eq. 2.4). Therefore H_a, the completion of compactly supported smooth functions in the Q norm, is the domain of the positive square root A_a^(1/2), with that norm, continuously embedded into L2. It is not the domain of A_a itself. The local Fourier image is contained in Paley–Wiener space PW_a; its domain is characterized by the integrability of (1+log^+|t|)|F[u](t)|^2. Point evaluation follows by Cauchy–Schwarz and coercivity. The two other de Branges axioms follow by reflection-conjugation and moving a nonreal zero to its conjugate: the latter preserves real-axis Fourier modulus and thus Q. Nonvanishing of some test transform at each given point precludes common zeros (Prop. 2.1).

The global completion H_W admits a unitary sampling map S to weighted ell2(Gamma,m), extending test-function Fourier samples (2.8; imported from Suzuki's cited Lemma 5.4). For each a, extending the isometric test-function inclusion gives j_a:H_a -> H_W with closed range M_a. These ranges nest and their union is dense because every test function is compactly supported. Thus P_a=j_a j_a* tends strongly to the identity. The adjoint j_a* is NOT ordinary pointwise restriction. Nor is a global model-space Fourier representative necessarily the local Fourier transform.

The global self-adjoint operator D is unitarily equivalent via S to multiplication by gamma on sequences. Its domain consists of c with sum_gamma m_gamma gamma^2 |c_gamma|^2 finite. In the alternate de Branges model of §2.3, the generating function is E(z)=xi(1/2-iz)+xi'(1/2-iz) and D corresponds to the phase-pi/2 multiplication extension (2.13). The spectral representation makes D explicit but uses zeros; it is the local construction that needs only finite arithmetic input.

## 2. The local extension (§3.1–3.2)
On H_a define the minimal operator T_a u=i u' on compactly supported smooth functions. Fourier transformation makes symmetry immediate on this domain: F[iu'](t)=t F[u](t). Use Riesz representatives r_z characterized by <u,r_z>=F[u](conj z); equivalently r_z=A_a^(-1)e^(-izx) in the operator domain of A_a, because the form identity recovers the L2 pairing.

Each adjoint eigenspace is span(r_z): the adjoint eigen-equation tested against smooth functions says the distribution u -> <u,f> has first derivative i conj(z) times itself, hence is a scalar multiple of evaluation at conj(z). Density identifies f. Therefore the two deficiency spaces are one-dimensional. Reflection R:u(x)->u(-x) is unitary and exchanges r_i and r_-i, so their norms agree.

Write Tbar for the closure of the minimal operator. Von Neumann extension domains are Dom(Tbar) algebraic-direct-sum span(r_i + exp(i theta) r_-i); the action is T_a* restricted to that domain. This is a nonlocal boundary condition in the form Hilbert space, not a periodic or antiperiodic endpoint condition in standard L2. Graph-norm convergence implies Dom(Tbar) is contained in H_0^1 by coercivity; the differential equation and zero traces imply Tbar has no eigenvectors. Reflection preserves exactly phases 0 and pi. The zero-eigenvector r_0 lies in the phase-0 domain by parity of its boundary pairing with r_i+r_-i; the distinct phase-pi domain therefore cannot contain it. See Lemma 3.1.

The phase-pi characteristic function can be evaluated from just two Riesz representatives:
    W_a(z)=(z-i)F[r_i](z)-(z+i)F[r_-i](z).
This follows from testing the adjoint boundary pairing against r_i-r_-i (3.3). Reflection gives evenness. Self-adjointness and nonzero evaluation vectors imply zeros are real eigenvalues. For a real root t, expand r_t in the extension domain as a minimal-domain vector plus c(r_i-r_-i), where c is nonzero since the minimal closure has no eigenvectors. The boundary pairing yields W_a(z)=(z-t)F[r_t](z)/c, so W_a'(t)=||r_t||^2/c is nonzero. This proves analytic simplicity, not just eigenspace dimension. Exponential type is at most a, using compact support and the L1 bound; W_a(0) is nonzero.

## 3. Inverse compression (Thm 3.3)
C=D^(-1) is bounded everywhere because zero is separated from Gamma. In the sampling basis its eigenvalues are 1/gamma, once per distinct gamma. The sum of their squares is finite, hence C is Hilbert–Schmidt, compact and self-adjoint.

Define C_a=j_a* C j_a, a bounded compact self-adjoint operator on H_a. For smooth compactly supported u, j_a u is in Dom(D) because Q(iu') is finite, and D j_a u=j_a T_a u. Therefore C_a T_a u=u. In particular the range of C_a contains a dense test core. Self-adjointness implies its kernel is zero; this is essential because compressing an injective indefinite operator is not generally injective.

Define L_a=C_a^(-1) on Ran(C_a). Its domain is dense; for h in Dom(L_a*) and k=L_a*h, testing with vectors C_a f gives <f,h>=<C_a f,k>=<f,C_a k>. Hence h=C_a k and L_a h=k. The inverse is therefore self-adjoint and extends the minimal differentiation. Its inverse is everywhere bounded, so 0 lies in its resolvent. Since R C R=-C and j_a intertwines reflection, L_a has a reflection-invariant domain and anticommutes with R. The only possible phases are 0 and pi; phase 0 has a zero eigenvalue, so L_a is phase pi. This proves the identity including domains:
    (T_{a,pi})^(-1)=C_a,  Dom(T_{a,pi})=Ran(C_a).

## 4. Convergence (Thm 1.1)
On the fixed global space, the lifted inverse is B_a=j_a C_a j_a*=P_a C P_a, which is C_a direct-summed with zero on M_a-perp. For a finite-rank approximation to C, strong convergence P_a->I gives Hilbert–Schmidt convergence on that approximation; the remainder is controlled uniformly by ||P_a||<=1. Thus B_a->C in Hilbert–Schmidt and operator norm. Infinite rank and reflection symmetry give infinitely many positive and negative local eigenvalues. Let mu_a,n denote the nth positive eigenvalue of C_a in descending order. The compact self-adjoint max–min principle applies even though C is indefinite. It maximizes the minimum Rayleigh quotient of C on n-dimensional subspaces of M_a. Nesting gives mu_a,n<=mu_b,n<=1/gamma_n for a<=b. Operator-norm convergence of B_a gives mu_a,n->1/gamma_n, by the same variational characterization and the bound on the difference of Rayleigh quotients. Reciprocation yields t_a,n>=gamma_n and nonincreasing convergence t_a,n->gamma_n, for fixed n. No rate in a follows just from strong projection convergence.

The transported local resolvent converges in norm on compact sets in the global resolvent via B_a(I-z B_a)^(-1) (Remark after Thm 1.1). This is not ordinary zero-extension of an unbounded local operator: the latter's resolvent has an additional complement term.

## 5. Entire-function limit (Thm 1.2, §5)
Hadamard factorization applies because W_a is even, nonzero at zero, entire of order at most one, with simple roots in +/- pairs. Grouping the linear exponential factors for each pair cancels them; evenness removes the remaining linear exponential. After normalization at zero the result is the product over positive t_a,n of (1-z^2/t_a,n^2). On |z|<=r, the product tails are controlled uniformly in a by r^2 times the tail sum of 1/gamma_n^2. The finite factors converge uniformly, hence the product converges locally uniformly to the distinct-zero product Delta_Gamma. No simplicity of zeta zeros has been used. Multiplicity weights change the Hilbert norm but each gamma is still a single coordinate, so they do not give extra spectral copies. Equality to xi(1/2-iz)/xi(1/2) requires simplicity (Cor. 5.2); the A/E limit of §5.2 is a different holomorphically normalized statement and does not recover multiplicities through the bare product.

## 6. R03 interface and guardrails
The finite Fourier spaces in §6 are form subspaces, not minimal differentiation domains. For finite Gram matrix G and transform vector phi(z), solve G conjugate(c(z))=phi(conj z) for z=+/-i. Substitute the resulting transforms in W_a, not the eigenvalues of G. The cutoff theorem concerns the exact local operator, not arbitrary finite Gram roots. Prop. 6.1 proves compact-uniform convergence at fixed a; finite roots need separate numerical and discretization checks.
