The form domain equals supported L2 functions with Fourier norm weighted by 1+log^+|t|: equivalence on the core follows from (4.5)-(4.6), and density in the supported weighted class follows by shrinking support with dilation r↑1 then mollifying inside the margin. Let F(z)=integral f(x)exp(izx)dx. If F(w)=0 for w nonreal, G=(z-conj(w))F/(z-w) is entire of the same exponential type, has equal real-axis modulus, and by Paley-Wiener is supported in the same window with the same weighted norm. Identically shrinking and mollifying both functions preserves modulus, hence autocorrelation and Weil energy on compact smooth tests. Passage to the form limit gives equal energy and L2 norms. This is unconditional, not the RH real-sample representation.

If f attains the spectral bottom, so does g, by the spectral theorem. Their difference has Fourier transform (w-conj(w))F/(z-w), proving bottom eigenspace closure under division by a nonreal zero. Removing k such zeros counted with multiplicity gives k+1 independent functions F,F/(z-w_1),...,F/prod(z-w_j), by clearing denominators (polynomials of distinct degrees). Thus each nonzero member of an m-dimensional bottom eigenspace has at most m-1 nonreal zeros counted with multiplicity. Evaluation at m distinct nonreal points, or derivatives of orders 0 through m-1 at one nonreal point, is injective. No nonreal point is a common zero, since repeated division removes finite vanishing order. Simple bottom has only real Fourier zeros; related to Connes–van Suijlekom's stated simple even case.
