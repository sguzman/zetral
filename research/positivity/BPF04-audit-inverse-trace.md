This is consistent with BPF02–03 (the opposite block carries its conjugate). This supplies the additive O(1) eigenvalue comparison; no assertion of a globally bounded real-zero operator. ## 2. Parity at every scale: exact pencil argument

Suppose the selected set is reflection symmetric, with u and -u at each deviation, equal multiplicities and no u=0. Let group k have h_k quartets (2h_k conjugate pairs). In the scaled synthesis of BPF03, physical reflection interchanges v_(L,u) and v_(R,-u) exactly. Thus its coefficient involution J interchanges these columns; E_a J=R E_a, G_a commutes with J and so does D_a. Each deviation block and the partitions into faster/current/slower indices are invariant. Restrict the pencil to either eigenspace of J, with the inherited Euclidean metric. The fast inverse bound and slow positive block argument apply without change. Each faster quartet contributes exactly one negative direction in each parity, because H_i acts by a signed Hermitian permutation on the parity coefficients (or by the paired block after identifying the two endpoints). Consequently the group k ordinary even and odd spectral depths, indexed after the sum of faster h_i in their respective sectors, are asymptotic to the negative and positive eigenvalue magnitudes of the Hermitian projected half-line coupling T_k(a), respectively, as in BPF02. To see the physical identification explicitly, the limiting projected left/right vectors are interchanged by endpoint swap with u->-u, so the limiting block is [0 T_k; T_k 0] with T_k self-adjoint. The reflection-invariant BV trial span proves the O(1) comparison on either sector separately. For the separated finite top set with an infinite remainder satisfying BPF03(5), the same sector restriction of the lower and upper form comparisons gives o(1) after rescaling. No continuity of parity eigenvalues is needed for this argument.

Let alpha_u=d_k+iu, beta_u the BPF03 product over all faster signed pairs, and let phi_u be the current left profile projected off the faster left span. Then phi_-u=conj(phi_u), beta_-u=conj(beta_u), and
    T_k(a)=sum_(u in group k) m_u exp(2iua) phi_u tensor phi_-u.
The trace of each rank-one term equals <phi_u,phi_-u>=beta_u^2/(2d_k+2iu) by the Cauchy formula in BPF03. This proves the trace identity for arbitrary finite current groups, not only one quartet.

## 3. Conditional inverse theorem from parity-resolved eigenvalue curves

Assume exact ordinary parity eigenvalue curves are given for all sufficiently large a, and assume the nonempty finite off-line hypothesis. Define N_(p,j)(a)=-lambda_(p,j)(A_a) for p=even,odd and for those fixed indices that are eventually strictly negative. By BPF03 and section 2, there are exactly sum h_k such indices in each parity and each has logarithmic growth rate
    d_k=lim_(a->infinity) log N_(p,j)(a)/(2a).
The grouping and counts are therefore determined by the data, even if the prefactors oscillate and eigenvalues within a group cross. For each recovered group define the scaled parity difference
    f_k(a)=exp(-2d_k a) [sum_(j in group k) N_(odd,j)(a)
                                -sum_(j in group k) N_(even,j)(a)].
The preceding sector asymptotics give
    f_k(a)=tr T_k(a)+o(1)
          =sum_(u in group k) c_u exp(2iua)+o(1),
    c_u=m_u beta_u^2/[2(d_k+iu)] !=0.                       (1)
Distinct signed ordinates at the same deviation give distinct frequencies; all coefficients are nonzero. For any real frequency omega and fixed sufficiently large a0 define
    C_k(omega)=lim_(T->infinity) (1/T) int_(a0)^(a0+T) f_k(a) exp(-i omega a) da.
The limit exists and equals c_u if omega=2u for an ordinate of this group, and zero otherwise. Indeed the average of each nonmatching exponential is bounded by 2/(T|2u-omega|), and the bounded eventual o(1) error has vanishing Cesaro mean by splitting off a fixed initial interval. Eigenvalue curves are measurable (physical inclusion yields monotone indexed eigenvalues), so the integrals are well-defined. No extraction of frequencies from individual ordered eigenvalue functions or rational-independence condition is required.

Proceed from the fastest group downward. The nonzero frequencies recover all signed u in the current group. With already recovered faster exponents, compute beta_u exactly; for the fastest group beta_u=1. Then
    m_u = 2(d_k+iu) C_k(2u)/beta_u^2.                     (2)
The result is a positive integer under the hypothesis, not a rounding prescription for noisy data. This recovers the whole finite set of hypothetical off-line zero pairs with multiplicities uniquely from the two parity-resolved ordinary spectral families, with the Fourier sign convention fixed. In zeta coordinates rho=1/2+iz, the pair at u±id gives real parts 1/2∓d and imaginary part u. The data do not determine critical-line zeros by this argument. In the separated finite top-set case with BPF03's weighted-density hypothesis, if the cutoff b is given, the selected groups are exactly the parity indices with growth limits exceeding b: the lower bound for the next eigenvalue bounds all further negative depths by O((a+1)^3 exp(2ba)). For indices not eventually negative use max(0,-lambda) and log(1+depth), which gives the same positive rates for the selected groups and limsup <=b for the remainder. Equations (1)-(2) then recover the selected set only. No finite-data algorithm, practical complexity, actual zero discovery or unconditional inverse theorem is asserted.

## 4. Limits and next question

The finite-data problem is substantive: the above mean limit uses arbitrarily large windows, exact spectral values, group identification, and nonzero complex coefficients which may become tiny from the product beta_u. A finite observation interval, unknown errors in the spectral depths, near-colliding ordinates and uncertainty in the exponential scaling can obstruct recovery. A quantitative finite-window theorem would turn the identifiability result into a conditional recoverability test and identify its stability limits. This is distinct from repeating the already derived growth rates. No guarantee that practical arithmetic computation of such windows is feasible.
