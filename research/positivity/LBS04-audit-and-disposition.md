## 1. Scoped audit

LBS01's log-energy identity follows from the Fourier multiplier exp(-s|t|) of the symmetric Cauchy probability kernel and Tonelli applied to the nonnegative difference integral. The kernel J is bounded by 1/(2|h|) near zero and 1/(2pi h^2) at infinity, so the bounded Lipschitz multiplier estimate follows by an integrable product-difference bound. With cutoff exp(-v x) on [-a,a], the norm bound is uniform apart from exp(2|v|(a+1)); the strip disk radius 1/a supplies a^2, and integration in v supplies 1/a. Thus the absolute strip square-sample estimate C a exp(2ba)L(f) has the stated linear polynomial factor. The weight is essential: this argument does not replace L(f) by L2 norm.

The inclusion from the finite support log-energy space into L2 is compact by frequency truncation plus a high-frequency tail, and the arithmetic form differs from L(f) by a bounded L2 form for fixed a. Consequently Q_a=I+compact; its nonpositive spectrum is finite and its negative index/nullspace agree with the ordinary form, not its nonzero eigenvalues. The separated selected remainder comparison is in the log norm and uses the classical local count alone.

LBS02 uses exact scaled evaluation Riesz vectors, not a restriction of the uncompressed Fourier multiplier inverse. Same-side projection convergence is strong on the increasing interval subspaces. ## 2. Disposition and a stronger metric

The main achievement is removal of an auxiliary density hypothesis for conditional detailed spectral recovery in the log metric. Remaining finite-set and gap assumptions are substantial. A barely stronger logarithmic weight could make the far-ordinate tail uniformly compact after boundary scaling, but it would still leave questions about summability of spectral traces for infinite leading sets. A stronger, more direct option is the H_0^1(-a,a) norm
    L_S(f)=||f||_2^2+||f'||_2^2.
The Dirichlet endpoint condition permits integration by parts in the evaluation functional, gaining a factor 1/|z| at high ordinates. Together with classical local logarithmic counts this suggests an absolute trace-class paired-zero expansion at every finite window. The positive metric inverse is the explicit Dirichlet Green function for -d^2/dx^2+1.

The specific next question is whether the reflection-weighted trace of this compact form operator admits an exact elementary formula for each zero ordinate, and whether a Laplace transform of the resulting parity trace recovers *all* ordinates (including possible infinitely many off-line pairs and nonattained extremal deviations). This is an exact trace problem, not merely an extrapolation of finite leading-mode prefactors. Its usefulness is conditional on obtaining arithmetic spectral trace data; it cannot be advertised as an RH proof or ordinary L2 spectral equivalence. Classical Green-function and trace methods are not themselves novel; compare relevant prior art before any originality claim.
