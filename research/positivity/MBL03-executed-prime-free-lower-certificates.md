# MBL03 — Executed one-cell lower-operator strict positivity certificates

2026-11-25. AI-assisted candidate computation and derivation, not independently reviewed. No RH assumption, actual off-line zero or priority claim. This is a full fixed-window positivity test for the specified prime-free intervals under the established operator identities, not merely a finite Ritz positivity calculation.

Use the one-cell parity reduction of MBL02 with physical length h=2a, h<log 2. Take the first J=512 positive boundary rates, and bound the even constant-to-complement weighted Schur correction by exact first N=64 mode coefficients and the explicit infinite tail. Odd complement positivity uses the explicit lower archimedean weights and the full negative pole norm. The Neumann digamma lower weights are those of DNB01; the rank correction is the exact finite lower operator from DNB02. All omitted rates are positive, so the conclusion transfers from B_J to the actual restored operator. No assumption of a numerical small operator-norm rate tail is used.

The source experiments/mbl03-one-cell-lower-certificate.js (commit 3d61f0057778f5c4eade329ab3261ba83ac95dcd) was actually executed in V8 and exactly read back. Arithmetic is outward-rounded BigInt at scale 10^18, with certified Machin pi and harmonic gamma bounds, logarithm via the atanh series, exp(-x) by halving to x<=1/2, alternating series, and repeated squaring. Intersect the exponential enclosure with [0,1]. Square root 2 endpoints are checked by exact squaring. The first moment entry H00 is evaluated including all J rate terms and the even pole, not just the Neumann baseline. For each positive even n<=64 compute the sum of rate and pole coefficients and square before dividing by the strictly positive w_n. The infinite weighted remainder is enclosed between zero and the explicit MBL02 tail upper endpoint. In particular the reported evenSchurLower interval encloses the value of H00 minus the true weighted comparison penalty, not the actual Schur eigenvalue; its lower endpoint is a lower bound on the exact scalar Schur complement. Both strict sign checks are required for full positivity.
