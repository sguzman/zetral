## Weak-direction coordinate selection

In the finite surrogate of RDC01, write b_{ia} for the correction linear coefficient for coordinate i and low direction a, and h_{ii} for the corresponding quadratic coefficient. When h_{ii}>0 a scalar maximization has gain |b_{ia}|²/h_{ii}. Rank coordinates by sum of this proxy over the first four approximate upper-energy eigenvectors, normalized by their positive midpoint energy. Guard the denominator by a small positive ridge; the selection remains heuristic. Use fixed-point gradients before converting scores to floating arithmetic so cancellation does not silently erase the weak directions. Select 18 highest scores, solve the same restricted ridge problem as RDC02, freeze rational coefficients and fully re-evaluate all outward intervals.

## Rational Cauchy allocation (different method)

Let the exact residual tail be the sum of seven signed scalar-frequency rows and eighth remainder, as in PPW03. For any positive shares p_i with sum_i p_i=1, weighted Cauchy gives
    (sum_i z_i)*(sum_i z_i) <= sum_i z_i* z_i / p_i
in matrix Loewner order (apply scalar Cauchy to any low vector). The previous shares were 1/2,1/4,1/8,1/16,1/32,1/64,1/128,1/128. If P_i denotes the corresponding already enclosed rank-one term or eighth diagonal allowance, the new tail majorant is sum_i (p_i^old/p_i) P_i. All factors are positive rational. The saved seven rank intervals are multiplied with signed directed floor/ceil; the eighth allowance is already a positive rational upper endpoint and can be scaled and rounded up. Intersect transposed intervals and subtract diagonal row radii as in AIR02. This uses the same operator, finite residual, exact rational basis and trial; no analytic recomputation or trust in a numerical optimizer. Any allocation with positive shares and sum one is admissible, even one selected from the stored data. For heuristic allocation, normalized trace of each unweighted Gram suggests shares proportional to square root of its trace; no optimality assumption is needed.

For comparing full lower matrices, fix the positive rational diagonal D of the *old* upper energy interval upper endpoints in this same rational basis. For a rational t, verify A_lower-t D>0 by exact integer Bareiss elimination after clearing denominators. This is a certified normalized margin of a sufficient lower comparison, not an ordinary spectral gap or the approximate relative diagnostic. The normalization is unchanged across allocations. Binary search on t=k/10000 returns a verified grid lower bound; no claim of the exact minimum eigenvalue. All candidate choices and winning exact lower matrices/minors preserved.
