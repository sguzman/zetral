## 1. Audit

TPH01 uses the classical PNT only for domination of the twisted measures by their positive untwisted limit. Their total variation is uniformly bounded and tight; the limiting densities have modulus at most one. The off-diagonal Hankel kernel has squared Hilbert-Schmidt norm int r exp(-r)|phi(r)|^2 dr <=1. The conjugate reverse block is necessary for complex twists. The compressed reflection operators have uniformly bounded norms, and fixed compact smooth tests give pointwise convergence with an exponential majorant, hence strong operator convergence (also of adjoints), not norm convergence. Truncating either endpoint at R removes shifts with r_n>=R, whose normalized weight is O(exp(-R/2)). For fixed demodulated envelopes, the normalized pole vanishes at divergent carriers, while the log carrier cost gives tau. Finite-dimensional trial approximation gives higher-index lower bounds but not upper bounds.

TPH02 is the central new candidate. The exact FES01 packet decomposition has support enlargement h on each side. The adjustable penalty delta exp(a)/C_2 guarantees a selected packet with loss <=2delta exp(a) and uniform normalized second carrier moment for fixed h,delta. The Fourier convention requires physical modulation by minus the packet Fourier center. Demodulation gives a uniform H1 bound. Local Rellich compactness in both endpoint coordinates suffices for expectation convergence because the arithmetic cross-block tail is uniformly small in norm. A possible loss of norm at the midpoint gives ||G||<=1, which is the correct inequality for the upper bound. Bounded carriers have identical prime and pole limits and cost zero, contradicting a strictly positive selected depth. Divergent carriers eliminate the pole by local strong convergence and the Riemann-Lebesgue lemma. The log symbol shift error is uniformly bounded using the second moment. Hence the limit packet depth is <D_phi G,G>-tau <=||K_phi||-tau. Taking the numerical inequality for every fixed h,delta before decreasing these parameters avoids an unjustified uniform compactness limit. This gives the equality for limsup exp(-a) N_1. It does not establish that the full limit exists, compute its value or prove a smaller exponential exponent.

TPH03 treats the norm-one case with care. The attainable set is closed by a diagonal realizing-sequence selection, but norm is only lower semicontinuous in the weak-measure topology; a maximum cannot be assumed in general. Bounded recentering of each realizing sequence, plus a vanishing carrier adjustment, preserves cost and multiplies phi by an arbitrary unit exponential. A sequence of near-maximal Hankel norms can first be recentered using a nonzero Fourier integral of its L1 kernel, since the convolution norm bounds the Hankel norm from above. This makes a weak limit nonzero. Strong convergence of both operators and adjoints, together with Hilbert-Schmidt error tending to zero after retaining a top singular-vector tensor, then forces both singular vectors to converge strongly up to common phases. Thus the limit really has norm one. Exact rank-one Hankel kernels satisfy the left-shift intertwining law; the corresponding scalar continuous multiplicative function gives exponential factors, with modulus-one density forcing real decay exponent -1/2. The attainable pair can then be made (1,0). The total twisted mass criterion follows by the elementary weighted mean-square phase identity, and removal of proper prime powers uses their O(log^2 X) total weight. This is a necessary-and-sufficient condition for saturation of coefficient one, not a proof of its occurrence or exclusion. ## 3. Disposition and next direction

The variational characterization makes the obstruction precise, but the attainable twisted phase set is not computed. Further abstract optimization without an arithmetic restriction on that set is unlikely to resolve the spectral exponent.

A distinct next route uses the paired explicit formula on two *separated* fixed smooth endpoint envelopes with a common physical modulation. Their archimedean cross term is a small off-diagonal kernel contribution, the normalized pole disappears for divergent carriers, and the paired zero sum carries factors exp[-a(1-2|Im z|)] times rapidly decaying carrier-relative test functions. This should connect every nonzero attainable twisted Hankel model to a carrier-centered weighted sum of zeros, rather than the global log norm. The classical zero-free region may then give an unconditional exclusion of moderate carriers substantially stronger than the untwisted PNT band from PBC03. Establish the exact cross identity and quantitative local counting/tail bounds; examine what they imply about actual off-line zero geometry and what remains unresolved at doubly exponential carrier scales. No claim that the proposed bounds are proved here.
