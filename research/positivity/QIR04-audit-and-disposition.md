## 1. Dependency and algebra checks

QIR01 upgrades the BPF03 pencil error using the finite-set Gram convergence, the inverse bound on the fast divided block, positivity of the slow divided block for negative thresholds bounded away from zero, and the fixed-set uniform gap in the effective finite spectrum. Each use is on a fixed finite selected set; constants can deteriorate with conditioning. The true operator comparison adds the positive real-zero contribution bounded on the BV trial span, or the separated lower remainder estimate under BPF03's uniform weighted local density hypothesis. Thus a finite rate exists conditionally, but its constants were not evaluated from arithmetic data. No numerical spectral error budget is yet available. The observation inequality uses absolute errors of the individual depths rather than a misleading relative error of a signed difference near zero. The triangular kernel is the Fourier transform of a probability convolution, so deterministic leakage is bounded by min(1,16/(T^2 x^2)). The grid localization uses both an upper amplitude sum and a positive lower amplitude bound: every true node has a mark, and marks from distinct nodes cannot share a cluster because r0<Delta/4. This is a sufficient margin, not an optimal resolution claim.

QIR02's Hankel factorization is a transpose factorization, not a Hermitian factorization. For q>=r the Vandermonde lower bound follows by the leading square rows; inverse Lagrange coefficient norms give the stated conservative bound. Although the rectangular product is not Hermitian, rank r and the lower bound on its rth singular value follow by restricting the right factor to its kernel complement. The square pencil formula is exact; the Neumann and diagonal similarity estimates give a one-sided root inclusion, and the homotopy in disjoint disks is needed for the bijective matching. The modulus-to-deviation map and argument-to-frequency map require a positive radial lower bound and a principal-argument guard. Uniform-grid aliases are a genuine obstruction in the generic exponential-sum model. None of these conditions follows merely from fitting small residuals. The coefficient estimate uses the Euclidean norm of the sample error and a finite Vandermonde perturbation bound. It is an enclosure conditional on validated input and numerical eigenvalue/linear solve bounds, not a report of a solver execution.

QIR03 uses the modulus rather than the absolute phase, with the gain exponent doubled again on squaring. The attenuation is a product of positive ratios of real sums of squares; faster multiplicities do not enter. Interval dependency is conservative, and a lower bound on the faster deviation gap can prevent a zero denominator in the final reciprocal attenuation. The exact integer-square test excludes every other positive integer when its interval contains only one square. The two recorded synthetic intervals were produced by exact rational arithmetic with explicitly assumed amplitude and gain enclosures; they do not verify QIR02's actual Hankel solver. For the broad interval the statement is only that this enclosure cannot exclude 2 or 3, not that there exist two actual zeta models with those observations. The common-gain ratio ambiguity is a magnitude-model ambiguity, not a general inverse spectral nonuniqueness theorem.

No new sign/factor error was identified in this scoped lead review; it is not independent verification.

## 2. What is and is not obtained

Given a finite separated group, bounded frequency band, certified amplitude/separation/geometry bounds and sufficiently precise parity depth observations, QIR01–03 supply a chain of sufficient enclosures for group scales, signed ordinates, and integer multiplicities. The finite error thresholds are explicit in these supplied quantities. Unknown group indices, a priori bounds, the implicit asymptotic constants, and actual validated eigenvalues of the arithmetic form remain separate requirements. Neither the conditional finite-pair assumption nor the weighted local density of a lower infinite remainder is known. No practical computation of those large-window observations was attempted. Classical windowing, Hankel-pencil recovery and interval propagation are not themselves priority claims.

The route has reached a clear bottleneck: improving the generic inverse-signal solver would not address the unproved uniform off-line density hypothesis or the unavailable arithmetic observations. Rather than add unneeded numerical variants, return to the forward spectral problem and see whether changing to the logarithmic-energy metric (already used for exact indexed growth exponents in NIR03) removes the lower-remainder density assumption at the level of detailed prefactors.
