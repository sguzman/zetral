## 2. Disposition

The vanishing-metric route has reached a concrete logical bottleneck: the current norm and graph estimates alone cannot prove a matching ordinary upper bound. Additional generic regularizations are unlikely to help without information specific to the arithmetic form.

A distinct next question is the large-window boundary scaling of the arithmetic prime-shift operator itself. The prime number theorem for psi(X)=sum_(n<=X)Lambda(n) suggests that after dividing by exp(a) and using endpoint coordinates s,t>=0, the weighted shifts near log n=2a-s-t converge to a rank-one kernel exp(-(s+t)/2). This is exactly the dominant cross-endpoint part of the pole form. A weighted Schur test may give sharp norm control, while the discrete shift structure may prevent operator-norm convergence even if strong convergence holds. Analyze this rather than assume the cancellation is uniform over frequency. In particular, finite collections of shifts admit arbitrarily high modulation recurrences; quantify the resulting norm defect separately from the logarithmic archimedean energy cost. If this works, it may reveal a precise spatial and frequency structure of possible ordinary negative modes at the scale exp(a), and an arithmetic-specific condition needed to go below that scale.
