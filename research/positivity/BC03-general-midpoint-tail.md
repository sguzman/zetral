## Higher-order derivation

Let r0=2J-1/2, x=omega^2, and f(r)=1/(r^2+x), d(r)=r^2/(r^2+x)^2, a(r)=2x/(r(r^2+x)). Tail samples are r0+2j+1. Shifted midpoint Euler–Maclaurin with K terms gives integral /2 minus sum of 2^(2k-1) B_(2k)(1/2) f^(2k-1)(r0)/(2k)! (and analogously for d,a). The periodic Bernoulli remainder has absolute value <=2^(2K-1)|B_(2K)| integral |f^(2K)|/(2K)!.

Use f=Laplace[sin(omega t)/omega], with the continuous omega=0 limit; d=(f+Re(r+i omega)^(-2))/2. The derivative bounds for each are (m+1)!/r^(m+2), hence the integrated EM remainder is <= 2^(m-1)|B_m|/r0^(m+1), m=2K. For a=2 Laplace[1-cos(omega t)], use 2(1-cos u)<=u^2 to obtain derivative bound x(m+2)!/r^(m+3), giving remainder <=2^(m-1)|B_m|(m+1)x/r0^(m+2). These hold uniformly in real omega. The exponentially weighted tails keep their previous bound Et. All derivatives decay at infinity and are integrable for the required EM order.

To avoid tiny intermediate powers, set y=x/r0^2 and (1+i omega/r0)^(-j)=A_j+i(omega/r0) C_j. Starting A0=1,C0=0, use A_(j+1)=(A_j+y C_j)/(1+y), C_(j+1)=(C_j-A_j)/(1+y). Set c_k=(1-2^(2k-1)) B_(2k)/(2k). Midpoint corrections at k for f,d,a are respectively -c_k C_(2k)/r0^(2k+1), c_k(2k A_(2k+1)-C_(2k))/(2 r0^(2k+1)), and 2c_k(1-A_(2k))/r0^(2k). Integrals are I/2, (I+r0/(r0^2+x))/4, log(1+x/r0^2)/2, with I=atan(omega/r0)/omega. Bernoulli numbers are generated by exact rational recurrence, coefficients include exact powers of rational r0 before conversion to the interval lattice. Outward rounding remains 10^66. This proof is internal candidate work, not a separately audited theorem.
