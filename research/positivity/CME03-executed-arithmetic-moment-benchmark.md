## Physical benchmark and proof of enclosures

Choose a=1/2, I=(-1/2,1/2), physical length 1, unit vector u=1 on I. Its zero extension is BV, hence belongs to the operator domains by RHI01/APM01. Only prime 2 contributes. Let d=x+1/2 and F(d)=int_d^infinity k(s)ds. Substitution y=exp(-d/2) gives F(d)=atanh(y)+atan(y), because 2/(1-y^4)=1/(1-y²)+1/(1+y²). The exact actions a.e. are
    T u(d)=alpha0+F(d)+F(1-d)
           -(log 2/sqrt 2)[1_(d<1-log 2)+1_(d>log 2)],
    A u(d)=T u(d)+8 sinh(1/4) cosh((d-1/2)/2).
The archimedean expression follows by applying the full-line difference kernel to a constant on I and zero outside. These actions are symmetric under d->1-d. The moments m1=<Bu,u> and m2=||Bu||² are therefore twice the integrals of Bu and (Bu)² over 0<d<1/2. In particular m2 uses the physical support; it is NOT the full-line Fourier squared-symbol integral.

Integrate these explicitly: int_0^h log(1/d)=h(log(1/h)+1), int_0^h log²(1/d)=h(log²(1/h)+2 log(1/h)+2). This controls the logarithmic boundary singularity rather than silently discarding endpoint cells.

## Independent periodic finite-cutoff benchmark

Take circle length 4 (L=2), frequencies t_n=pi n/2, and a sharp cutoff of the arithmetic distribution at |s|<=1. Its discontinuities are away from the singular origin and outside every compact test autocorrelation; hence the compressed form is exact as in CPC04. For the archimedean symbol expand k(s)=sum_(j>=0) exp(-r_j s), r_j=2j+1/2. Direct integration of alpha0+2 int_0^1 k(s)(1-cos ts)ds+2 int_1^infinity k(s)ds gives
    alpha_chi(t)=alpha0+2 sum_j [t²/(r_j(r_j²+t²))
                  + exp(-r_j)(r_j cos t-t sin t)/(r_j²+t²)].
The positive main tail after J terms is <=t²/[8(J-1)²], from r_j>=2j and integral comparison. The absolute exponential tail is <=2 exp(-r_J)/(r_J(1-exp(-2))). Subtract 2(log 2/sqrt 2)cos(t log 2). Add for A the pole symbol 4[(1/2)sinh(1/2)cos t+t cosh(1/2)sin t]/(1/4+t²). At sampled frequencies cos t and sin t are exact integer signs/zeros. The remaining prime cosine is enclosed by reducing its angle by an integer multiple of 2pi and taking 40 Taylor terms plus the first omitted term (the tail is alternating decreasing from that index for reduced magnitude <=2pi+tiny rounding). The coefficient c_n of u is sin(pi n/4)/t_n for n!=0, c_0=1/2. Q_ij=sin(pi(i-j)/4)/(pi(i-j)) off diagonal and 1/4 on diagonal. All sine values are exact rational/sqrt2 intervals. Compute m1_N=c_N*D_N c_N, m2_N=(D_N c_N)*Q_N(D_N c_N). This explicitly retains the support projection between powers. J=256 for the archimedean series.
