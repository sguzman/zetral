## 1. Audit of the full indexed converse

HPS01's joint positive measure gives moment densities dominated by the marginal and pointwise Toeplitz positivity via a countable dense set of coefficient vectors. The k=0 channel is excluded because the pole cancels the prime model at bounded carrier. Finite-channel Gram orthogonality follows from Riemann-Lebesgue, while the normalized cross prime terms use strong convergence in one modulated channel against a weak-null other channel. The archimedean shift estimate is in L2 action on fixed smooth envelopes, so its normalized cost contributes only same-channel scalar terms. HPS02 therefore transfers finite dimensions to actual ordinary trial spans and reflection-symmetrized spans; no auxiliary packet overcount is used. The norm-one harmonic result uses the actual attainment of (1,0) proved in TPH03, so all fixed-index saturation statements hold on one common sequence.

For HPS03, the short-endpoint weighted Schur estimate is important: restricting one endpoint to (0,delta) has norm bounded in limsup by sqrt(1-exp(-delta)), using the uniform PNT row bound on the other endpoint. This, the large-endpoint tail and vanishing diagonal prime blocks justify the smooth cut-off approximation in norm, despite the unrestricted prime-pole cancellation defect. The cutoff supports lie inside the physical intervals for sufficiently large a. The archimedean midpoint split is justified for smooth f by first trimming/mollifying each zero-extended half restriction in log norm (as in VMR02), then passing the disjoint-support digamma kernel identity to the limit. The cross kernel at the midpoint is bounded on L2 by the uniform Carleman Schur bound C/(u+v), not by a pointwise bounded kernel. Applying the resulting norm inequality to smooth differences extends the split continuously to the original form domain. Thus the enlarged-space comparison does not silently require a boundary trace at the midpoint. The diagonal log multiplier is positive and the localized bounded term is relatively compact at each fixed window, so a transferred strictly negative subspace supplies actual enlarged negative eigenvectors.

The integrated Fourier localization identity uses all continuous band centers, with exact mass and diagonal-form decomposition. The commutator with the fixed spatial cutoff has integrated square-norm O(L^-2) by Minkowski and the L2 translation estimate of the band function. The full-line convolution multiplier commutes with the band cutoff; the fixed cut-off pole has a Schwartz Fourier kernel so the same integrated commutator estimate holds. The localization error is O(1/L), independently of a. Enlarged negative eigenvectors have a uniform Fourier H1 bound by their spectral equation: the bounded term has fixed compact physical output support and the reciprocal diagonal-minus-negative-eigenvalue multiplier has bounded value and derivative. This supplies strong local Fourier convergence near any previously chosen carrier. The sign of the physical modulation differs from the Fourier center due to the +itx transform convention; oriented endpoint modulation also differs by a constant relative endpoint phase. HPS03 explicitly takes a subsequence of that scalar and uses unitary equivalence of the resulting blocks. Bounded carriers have residual limit zero. Divergent carriers have the compact cut-off Hankel block as their strong limit.

The main dimension argument is sound at the level of this scoped review: all original enlarged eigenvector weak limits at a chosen carrier satisfy the model eigen-equation, hence lie in its above-threshold finite eigenspace. Orthogonality to its translated basis imposes at most that many linear constraints on the actual j-dimensional enlarged negative subspace. If the total count is <j, a unit vector satisfying all constraints has strong local Fourier limit zero near all already chosen carriers. A slowly growing forbidden neighborhood then carries vanishing packet mass. The integrated localization identity finds a negative band packet outside it, with center diverging from all prior centers. Its normalized band support supplies physical H1 compactness on the fixed cutoff supports, its log cost is bounded, and a new nonzero above-threshold model eigenspace follows. The process terminates after at most j steps. The packet vectors need not be orthogonal and are not counted as such. The actual counts are model eigenspace dimensions, and each cutoff-model positive threshold subspace injects under the spatial contraction into the uncut model at the same threshold. This gives HPS03(5), with all approximation parameters fixed inside the desired threshold before extracting subsequences. No growing-index bound or evaluated arithmetic coefficient follows.

## 2. Parity-indexed upper characterization

The enlarged proof can be made reflection symmetric. In oriented doubled coordinates physical reflection is the unitary involution
    J(g_L(t),g_R(t))=(g_R(-t),g_L(-t)).
The diagonal multiplier is even and the spatial cutoffs are reflections of one another. Reflection interchanges the off-diagonal convolution by mu_(a,0) with its adjoint (reflection reverses a real shift), so the full enlarged operator commutes with J. The pole block also commutes. Thus the comparison and minmax transfer hold separately in either parity.

Define an admissible positive separated family by the HPS03 conditions with all physical carriers positive eventually. The limits of the costs and densities along the opposite carriers are their conjugate counterparts with the same costs and singular values. From the general finite-channel convergence in HPS03 section 1, the reflection-symmetrization argument of HPS02 works for any such positive family, not merely harmonics. For each fixed parity index j its liminf scaled depth is >=c along the family's windows whenever the positive family alone has at least j gains >c.

For the upper bound, start with j enlarged eigenvectors in the specified parity at normalized depth >=c_0, exactly as in HPS03. Choose the band function b even, which is possible in its localization identity. In the extraction, keep the Fourier centers positive, allowing reflection of a negative-center packet: the reflected packet has the same norm and form value because g_l has definite parity, b is even and the enlarged form is reflection invariant. In oriented coordinates J reverses the Fourier center and swaps components. Every selected positive center therefore has an opposite-center model with the reflected eigenspace. For the dimension constraints on the chosen parity subspace it suffices to impose orthogonality to the translated model space at the positive center: orthogonality to its reflected opposite-center space follows automatically from Jg_l=±g_l. Thus the cost in dimension is dim Y_r, not twice dim Y_r.

In HPS03 section 5 the forbidden neighborhoods now include BOTH signs of every prior center. The local weak limits at the opposite signs vanish by reflection of the already constrained limits at the positive signs, and so do their local strong Fourier norms. The same packet-selection estimate applies with this finite union of neighborhoods. Reflect any selected negative center to the positive side, preserving its distance from all existing signed neighborhoods. A bounded center is still impossible because its residual model is zero. All new positive absolute centers therefore diverge and are pairwise separated. A new cut-off positive-gain eigenspace is obtained exactly as before, and each iteration costs its positive-center dimension in the parity subspace. After at most j steps the positive family has at least j uncut model gains above the target threshold. This proves the parity counterpart:
    limsup exp(-a) N_(even,j)(a)
      = limsup exp(-a) N_(odd,j)(a)
      = sup_{finite jointly realized positive separated families}
          sup{c>0 : sum_r #{i:sigma_i(K_phi_r)>tau_r+c}>=j}.  (1)
All suprema are zero if the threshold set is empty. The proof is for fixed j and ordinary L2. It does not claim finite-window equality of the parity eigenvalues.

## 3. Full/parity duplication, even along arbitrary window sequences

The full family in HPS03 may include positive and negative carriers. For any fixed finite such family, pass to a subsequence on which each sign is constant and each pair of absolute carrier differences either converges to a finite number (with signed difference after absolute value also convergent) or diverges in magnitude. Partition by bounded absolute differences, an equivalence relation by the triangle inequality. Because the original signed carriers are separated, each class contains at most two channels, at most one of each sign. In a class with two channels, reflection of the negative one gives a positive carrier differing from the positive representative by a bounded shift tending to a real number. Their log costs agree. Their twisted measure limits differ by multiplication by a unit exponential, after a further subsequence of the constant phase exp(2ia*shift); by TPH03 section 1 this does not change the Hankel singular values. (The same conclusion follows directly from uniform convergence on compact r tests of the bounded shift factor.) Thus each class contributes at most twice the count of one positive representative. The positive representatives of distinct classes are separated. Conversely every positive separated family can be doubled by adding its reflected negative carriers, which are all pairwise separated from the positives and from each other, with the same singular values and costs.

It follows from HPS03(5) and (1) that for every fixed j,
    limsup exp(-a) N_(2j-1)(a)
      = limsup exp(-a) N_(2j)(a)
      = limsup exp(-a) N_(even,j)(a)
      = limsup exp(-a) N_(odd,j)(a).                    (2)
In fact there is the stronger asymptotic equality of these four scaled quantities as a->infinity, in the sense that their pairwise differences tend to zero, without requiring that their common-scale value has a limit. To justify this upgrade rather than infer it merely from equal limsups, take any sequence of windows and extract a subsequence on which the four bounded scaled depths converge. The extraction proofs above apply on ANY such sequence whose relevant depth has a positive lower margin, giving a positive separated family on a subsubsequence at any strictly smaller threshold. The finite-channel lower bound then transfers that threshold to both parities and to the corresponding full even index on the same subsubsequence. If the starting depth is the full (2j-1)st or 2jth, the grouping argument converts its at least 2j-1 gains into at least j positive-family gains, since a group accounts for at most twice its positive count. If the starting depth is a parity jth, its positive-family count at least j gives both signs and hence 2j full gains. Thus any one of the four subsequential limiting depths greater than another would give a threshold strictly between them and a contradiction on a common subsubsequence. They must all be equal. Compactness of the bounded scalar sequences proves the claimed pairwise o(1) differences on the full expanding-window limit.

This is an unconditional candidate structural theorem at the scale exp(a), even if the coefficient is zero. At smaller exponential scales, genuine finite off-line groups can have unequal, oscillating parity prefactors (BPF02), so (2) must NOT be extrapolated to a relative equality at their scales. No actual nonzero ordinary coarse coefficient is established.
