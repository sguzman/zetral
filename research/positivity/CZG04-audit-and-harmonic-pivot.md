Taking absolute values gives the exact attenuation exp(-2a(1-beta)) and arbitrary fixed inverse-power carrier-relative decay. The local zero count yields the logarithmic total weighted bound and a tail of order (log(2+|omega|)+log(2+R))(1+R)^(1-p), p>1. ## 2. Disposition

The carrier-centered estimates locate the obstruction in an explicit weighted local nonreal-zero mass. The double-exponential regime still permits that mass under the available count and zero-free estimates. Global density controls rarity, not pointwise exclusion. Further variants of those same inequalities are not a promising route to an ordinary upper exponent.

A distinct indexed question follows from TPH03's global saturation criterion. If S=1, the weighted prime phases align at some divergent carrier omega with zero log cost. Then every fixed integer harmonic k omega also has nearly constant weighted phases, because |z^k-c^k|<=|k| |z-c| for unit phases. All nonzero harmonics have the same limiting normalized log cost (zero in saturation). More generally one can simultaneously extract the twisted phase limits of all harmonics, as moments of a probability kernel on the phase circle conditional on r. Distinct physical modulations become weakly orthogonal on fixed smooth endpoint envelopes, and TPH01's strong limit for each conjugated prime block should make the cross Rayleigh terms vanish. This could assemble finite spans of different carrier channels and prove genuine higher-index lower bounds for the original ordinary operator, rather than the bottom-only upper estimate of TPH02. In saturation it suggests that every fixed negative index (including both parities) reaches the same coarse coefficient along a common realizing sequence. This is a proposed next theorem, not proved in this audit. A full higher-index converse would need a rigorous multi-packet/profile argument and is not presumed.
