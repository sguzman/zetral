## 1. Audit of boundary cancellation

PBC01's ordinary L2 split at the midpoint is an isometry onto two zero-extended intervals; unlike the earlier H1 split it imposes no midpoint condition. Each cross shift occurs once, at t+s=2a-log n, with normalized coefficient exp(-a)Lambda(n)/sqrt(n). Each same-half block has norm O(exp(-a/2)) after normalization. Multiplying by h(s)/h(t), h(t)=exp(-t/2), changes the cross coefficient exactly to Lambda(n)/exp(2a-t). The positive weighted Schur bound therefore has constant sup_(X>=exp a)psi(X)/X ->1, not a total-mass factor of two. The weak measure limit exp(-r/2)dr follows by partial summation of the classical PNT. On fixed compact smooth profiles the pointwise limit has the integrable majorant C_g h(t); uniform boundedness then gives strong convergence on all half-line L2. This proves the limiting norm of the normalized prime operator is one, without an unrestricted operator-norm approximation.

PBC02's cross pole kernel is h(t)h(s)+exp(-2a+(t+s)/2), with second rank-one norm O(exp(-a)); the same-half pole blocks are o(1). Thus the pole converges in norm to the same rank-two block as the prime strong limit. On span h and its complement, the cross norm defect is at most 1+o(1) since K_a h->h and ||K_a||<=1+o(1). The finite-shift modulation recurrence preserves the prime operator up to arbitrarily small norm error at fixed a, while compact operators annihilate any fixed modulated weak-null vector. Hence the essential norm of the finite-window prime operator equals its norm, and the pole subtraction cannot lower this essential norm. This gives the reverse limit, so the normalized prime-minus-pole norm tends exactly to one although its strong limit is zero. There is no quantitative recurrence bound relative to the archimedean log-energy cost. The ordinary form lower bound follows from alpha>=alpha(0), giving limsup exp(-a)N_1<=1, not an attained spectral coefficient or a smaller exponent.

## 2. Audit of deep ordinary subspace localization

PBC03's interior cutoff removes every shift longer than 2a-R; the remaining normalized prime and pole norms are O(exp(-R/2)). The archimedean commutator has integrable majorant min(s,1)k(s) because the cutoff is uniformly Lipschitz. The log form domain is invariant under the cutoff, and the bounded commutator identity gives the corresponding operator-domain invariance. The Sylvester equation on the finite-dimensional negative spectral subspace integrates across the gap alpha(0)+c exp(a)>0, so its estimate applies to the entire subspace without a hidden dimension factor. This establishes uniform endpoint tightness of hypothetical eigenstates at depth at least c exp(a).

The even signed measure for the bounded arithmetic residual is an exact compressed convolution on the zero-extended physical interval. Splitting the positive shifts at n=exp(a), Stieltjes summation of psi(x)-x gives the low-frequency multiplier bound C min(1,eta_a(1+|t|)). The endpoint truncation of a bandlimited function creates two delta terms in its derivative; both were included in the Fourier tail estimate O(R/S+sqrt(R/S)). The full log-squared multiplier norm bound follows from w(t)/t decreasing on t>=1 and the integral of w(t)^2/t^2. The resulting restricted band-limited vectors are in the archimedean operator domain. Optimizing S=(R/eta_a^2)^(1/3) balances the residual error and the endpoint truncation tail, and the spectral projection estimate yields a norm bound on the whole deep negative subspace, not only one expectation estimate. The imported Korobov–Vinogradov rate gives an explicit class of growing cutoff scales with an unevaluated positive constant. This is far weaker in frequency scale than exclusion of doubly exponential escape. Strong cancellation on fixed boundary profiles also proves weak-null behavior of the boundary coordinates uniformly over the subspace.

## 3. Disposition and next arithmetic-specific question

Campaign XXV has established a sharper unconditional coarse ordinary bound and a structural obstruction to a naive continuous PNT approximation: strong cancellation, but an exact normalized norm defect of one. Deep ordinary eigenstates, if present, must be endpoint-tight while losing all mass on growing moderate-frequency bands. The remaining issue is whether the arithmetic shifts can sustain such a state without excessive logarithmic archimedean cost.

A sharper question near the normalized upper constant one is now available. With h(t)=exp(-t/2), write left/right endpoint profiles g_L=h v_L, g_R=h v_R. The weighted edge measure for the cross shifts is
    dGamma_a(t,s)=sum_(n) exp(-2a)Lambda(n) 1_(0<t,s<a, s=2a-log n-t) dt
where each summand denotes pushforward of dt to the stated segment. Its marginals are h^2(t) times the row factor from PBC01; in fact this row factor is 1+o(1) uniformly, by subtracting psi(exp(a-t)) and using PNT for the upper endpoint exp(2a-t). The cross Schur deficit should therefore be the weighted integral of |v_L(t)-v_R(s)|^2 plus a small row error. This can give a useful near-extremal rigidity identity. The conditional distribution of one endpoint given the other is an elementary prime-weight distribution, and its action on fixed continuous tests should converge uniformly to the exponential probability density. If the deficit tends to zero, this suggests a universal limiting spatial intensity even though the wavefunctions themselves tend weakly to zero. Verify these steps and then examine the frequency/prime phase implications of the weighted difference identity.
