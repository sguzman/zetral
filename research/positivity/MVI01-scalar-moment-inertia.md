# MVI01 — Exact scalar moment inertia and visible parity spectra

2026-11-25. AI-assisted candidate deduction without RH. Classical background checked this turn by actual search: *Structural properties of Krylov subspaces, Krylov solvability, and applications to unbounded self-adjoint operators*, arXiv:2404.19698 (abstract explicitly relates Krylov approximation and Hamburger determinacy); *Measure-theoretic bounds on the spectral radius of graphs from walks*, DOI 10.1016/j.laa.2021.04.023 (search excerpt identifies shifted Hankel positivity and the Stieltjes support criterion). No general novelty claim, no full-paper verification.

Let B be either actual parity operator T or A, and u its pole vector, analytic by APM01. Denote distinct eigenvalues lambda, orthogonal spectral projections E_lambda, and mu=sum_lambda ||E_lambda u||² delta_lambda. The cyclic subspace C is the closed span of p(B)u for all polynomials p. Its spectral model is L²(mu): the map sending a scalar function f to f(B)u is an isometry onto the closed span of the nonzero E_lambda u. Polynomials are dense in L²(mu) and in L²((1+|lambda|)mu) by the exponential-moment signed-measure argument of APM03 (translate by a lower spectral bound if desired). Thus C equals that span, reduces B, and polynomial vectors form a core for the closed form restricted to C. At each visible distinct eigenvalue its multiplicity in C is exactly one, not its full multiplicity in the parity sector.

For any real threshold z define H_n=(m_(i+j)) and L_n(z)=(m_(i+j+1)-z m_(i+j)), i,j=0,...,n, with m_k=<B^k u,u>. The two matrices represent the Gram and shifted form of polynomial vectors p(B)u. The negative inertia of L_n(z) is the dimension of a maximal strictly negative subspace of this finite form, including if H_n is singular: a null vector of H_n represents the zero vector and lies in ker L_n. By the min-max principle on C this inertia is at most
    r(z)=#{distinct lambda<z with E_lambda u !=0}.
It is nondecreasing in n by nested polynomial spaces. Conversely approximate the r(z) normalized negative spectral vectors in the restricted form norm by polynomial vectors using the weighted density just proved. Since r(z) is finite (compact resolvent), the resulting Gram and shifted form matrices converge to the identity and a strictly negative diagonal matrix. For all sufficiently large n these approximants span a negative subspace of dimension r(z). Therefore
    n_-(L_n(z))=r(z) for all sufficiently large n.
This is finite exact exhaustion at each fixed threshold, not a claim of an effective n bound. Zero eigenvalues at the threshold do not count as negative. All L_n(z) positive semidefinite iff no visible eigenvalue is below z. Ritz values on the polynomial spaces decrease to the visible indexed eigenvalues by the same form-density argument and min-max, whenever the corresponding dimension exists.

## Rank-one restoration preserves the cyclic subspace

For T pole-free and A=T+sigma u tensor u, induction on k gives
    A^k u=T^k u+sum_(j<k) c_(k,j) T^j u.
Each step is legal by analyticity/domain membership in APM01; applying the bounded rank-one term gives a multiple of u. The coefficient of T^k u is one, so the finite Krylov spaces coincide for every degree, and hence so do their closures C. Both operators reduce C and C^perp, and on C^perp their actions and domains coincide since the rank-one perturbation vanishes there. On C both have simple eigenvalues by the cyclic spectral representation. They share no eigenvalue on C: if T has eigenvalue lambda with E_lambda u !=0 and A f=lambda f in C, projecting the equation onto E_lambda gives sigma E_lambda u <f,u>=0. Since sigma !=0 this forces <f,u>=0, so T f=lambda f; but on C this eigenspace is spanned by E_lambda u and has nonzero overlap with u, a contradiction for nonzero f.

Consequently the full parity relative count difference from RHI03 is exactly the count difference on C; all common uncoupled eigenvalues cancel, even if their values coincide with active ones. The infinite relative heat germ recovers the T pole moments by APM02 and hence its visible eigenvalues and weights by APM03 determinacy. It also recovers the active A eigenvalues from the relative count difference (or the rank-one secular identity). It does NOT recover the multiplicities or locations of the unchanged spectrum on C^perp. The positive even T ground state is visible by AJP01 because c>0; no such claim for every higher eigenmode or the odd sector. Thus localizing moments give finite witnesses of visible negativity but cannot by themselves certify absence of full negative spectrum.
