## 1. Fixed-window resolvent limit without RH

Fix a>0. Write T=A_a-lambda_1(a)I, E=ker T of dimension m, V its form domain, H=V/E with q_T norm. Identify H with V intersect E^perp (orthogonality in L2), using the gap g>0. For s>0 write V_s=V with q_s=q_T+s||.||_2^2. The minimal differentiation S_s is the closure of if' on compact smooth tests in V_s. Its deficiencies at ±i are one dimensional by the evaluation-functional argument of QER01 (or Suzuki §6.2–6.4); D_s is ANY self-adjoint extension, phase may depend on s. D is QER01's unique self-adjoint quotient differentiation. Work in the fixed Hilbert space K=H direct sum E, E with L2 norm. The unitary
    U_s f = (L_s[f], sqrt(s) P_E f), L_s=(I+s T^+)^(1/2) on H
follows from spectral calculus on E^perp, since q_T(x,T^+x)=||x||_2^2. In particular L_s tends to I in operator norm, as T^+ bounded by 1/g. Let R_s(z)=U_s(D_s-z)^-1 U_s^-1 and R(z)=(D-z)^-1 for z nonreal.

For any test f, U_s f -> ([f],0) and U_s(if'-zf) -> ((D-z)[f],0) in K. The exact resolvent identity on that test and uniform bound 1/|Im z| show
    R_s(z)(x,0) -> (R(z)x,0)
on the dense subset x=(D-z)[f] of H (graph core by definition), and hence on all H. All errors are independent of the extension phase. Same for bar z, i.e. the adjoints.

## 2. Upgrade to norm on the quotient component

We show sequential collective compactness of the transported resolvents as s decreases to zero, uniformly in phase. A bounded graph-norm sequence of minimal-domain vectors can be approximated by smooth tests f_s with error tending to zero in the s-graph norm. For the tests split k_s=(iD_x-i)f_s=g_s+e_s with g_s perpendicular to E in L2 and e_s in E. Bounds on q_s of f_s and if_s' imply bounds on q_T of their classes and on ||g_s||_2 via the gap. The left-zero Volterra inverse V_i of iD_x-i on the interval maps L2 boundedly into H1(-a,a). It maps compactly into the supported log Fourier form domain: its integral kernel is Hilbert-Schmidt on the square, and integration by parts on the interval bounds the zero-extended Fourier tail by C||g||_2/|t| for |t|>=1, including the endpoint traces. Thus bounded sequences have convergent L2 subsequences and uniformly vanishing weighted log tails, hence convergence in form norm. The left boundary condition gives f_s=V_i g_s+V_i e_s. In H, [V_i g_s] is precompact and [V_i e_s] bounded (difference of bounded terms), in a fixed finite-dimensional space, hence precompact. Since L_s->I and sqrt(s)P_E f_s is bounded in finite dimension, U_s f_s is precompact in K.

For full extension domains use von Neumann's graph-orthogonal decomposition into minimal-domain vector and one-dimensional extension complement in the sum of the ±i defect spaces. A unit V_s defect vector at z=±i is the normalized (T+s)^-1 e_z, e_z(x)=exp(-izx) as in Suzuki. Its squared q_s norm is ||P_E e_z||_2^2/s+< (T|Eperp+s)^-1 (I-P_E)e_z,(I-P_E)e_z >. U03 gives P_E e_z nonzero for each nonreal z. The transported normalized vector therefore has H component O(sqrt(s)), E component bounded. Its graph norm is sqrt(2); graph-orthogonal decomposition (including the orthogonality of the two defects in the adjoint graph norm) bounds coefficients of the two normalized defect vectors independently of phase. Hence all graph-bounded extension sequences have precompact images as s->0. Resolvent outputs of bounded inputs have bounded graph norms uniformly in s and phase, since D_s(D_s-z)^-1=I+z(D_s-z)^-1. This also applies at bar z.

Let P_H, P_E be projections in K. Strong convergence on H for both resolvent and adjoint, together with the just-proved compactness, implies
    ||P_H R_s(z)|H-R(z)|| ->0,
    ||P_H R_s(z)|E||+||P_E R_s(z)|H|| ->0.          (QER03.1)
For the first assertion, take any sequence of unit H inputs, pass to weakly convergent subsequence, and use precompactness of outputs and testing against fixed H vectors with the strongly convergent adjoints to identify the H limit; compactness of R gives the same strong limit on the varying inputs. For the H-from-E block, the same testing with adjoints gives weak limit zero and precompactness gives norm convergence; use adjoints at bar z for the other block. Contradiction-sequence reasoning makes these statements uniform over all phases. This does not claim norm convergence of the entire R_s on K.

Every sequence s->0 with arbitrary phases has a subsequence for which the finite block P_E R_s(i)|E converges in norm to a matrix C; by (QER03.1) the full resolvent at i then converges in norm to R(i) direct sum C. The limit is compact normal, and C-C*=2i C*C by the resolvent identity. Its eigenvalues are 0 or (t-i)^-1 for real t; zero allows escape to infinity. Spectral projections onto isolated nonzero eigenvalues of the limiting compact normal operator converge in norm (contour resolvent identity). Thus every edge eigenvalue has nearby positive-metric eigenvalues uniformly in phase as s->0, and in any compact interval disjoint from the edge spectrum there are at most m additional eigenvalues counted with multiplicity for all sufficiently small s: otherwise pass to a subsequence as above and isolate the finitely many nonzero limiting points on the resolvent image of the interval, all from C. This finite-block statement does not identify the extra modes or their phase trajectories; no uniform-in-a estimate obtained.

## 3. Large windows: conditional weighted measure convergence

Assume RH ONLY in this section. The source sampling identity on compact smooth tests is q_a(f,g)=sum_gamma m_gamma F[f](gamma) conj(F[g](gamma)), for support inside (-a,a), where gamma ranges over distinct real ordinates. Use the standard local zeta zero count with multiplicity O(log(2+|t|)) on each unit interval, from Riemann–von Mangoldt; this is an imported classical estimate. The gap around t=0 (xi(1/2)!=0) may instead be replaced by ANY fixed t0 not in the discrete ordinate set. Choose unit L2 test phi supported in (-1,1), f_a(x)=a^-1/2 phi(x/a) exp(-it0 x). Fourier transform sqrt(a) F[phi](a(t-t0)). Schwartz decay and positive distance from t0 to the ordinates bound q_a(f_a,f_a)=O_N(a^(1-2N)) for N>1, using convergence of sum_gamma m_gamma |gamma-t0|^-2N. Since RH implies lambda_1(a)>0, minmax gives lambda_1(a)->0.

QER02 supplies positive measures mu_a=sum_(t in spectrum D_a) kappa_t^-1 delta_t. For fixed compact smooth f,g and all sufficiently large a,
    integral F[f] conj(F[g]) dmu_a
      = q_W(f,g)-lambda_1(a)<f,g>_2.                (QER03.2)
Let mu_zeta=sum_gamma m_gamma delta_gamma. For a fixed compact smooth test psi with F[psi](0)!=0 choose epsilon>0 and c>0 so |F[psi]|>=c on [-epsilon,epsilon]. For its modulation psi_u(x)=exp(-iux)psi(x), (QER03.2) gives
    mu_a([u-epsilon,u+epsilon]) <= c^-2 q_W(psi_u,psi_u)
      <= C log(2+|u|),
uniform in a large enough to contain the fixed support. The first inequality uses lambda_1(a)>=0. The second follows by summing Schwartz decay against the unit-interval zero count, using log(2+|u+k|)<=log(2+|u|)+log(2+|k|). Cover unit intervals by finitely many epsilon intervals to obtain uniform polynomial (indeed logarithmic) local growth.

Every sequence a->infinity has a vaguely convergent subsequence of these positive locally bounded measures (diagonal compactness of Radon measures). The uniform growth bound carries to the limit and allows passage of integrals of Schwartz functions by uniformly small tails. For a vague limit mu, (QER03.2) and lambda_1(a)->0 imply equality with mu_zeta on products F[f] conj(F[g]). Take g=psi_delta, a compact smooth approximate identity with integral one, L1 norm bounded, and support shrinking to zero; its Fourier transform tends to 1 pointwise and is uniformly bounded. Dominated convergence gives equality on F[f] for every compact smooth f. The difference of measures is a tempered distribution by the growth bound; Fourier transforms of compact smooth tests determine it (Fourier injectivity, or density in Schwartz space). Hence mu=mu_zeta. All subsequences have this unique limit, proving
    mu_a -> mu_zeta vaguely under RH.              (QER03.3)

For compact intervals with endpoints off Gamma, total spectral weights converge to summed zeta multiplicities. Every ordinate has quotient spectral nodes in every fixed neighborhood for all sufficiently large windows. There is no proof that all nodes converge to ordinates, that the number of local nodes in a cluster equals one or the multiplicity, or that individual weights converge: vanishing-weight spurious nodes are compatible with vague convergence. This is distinct from a noncircular RH proof and from the RH conditional phase-pi eigenvalue convergence already known. No independent numerical/formal verification, no novelty claim. QER04 consolidate and pivot.
