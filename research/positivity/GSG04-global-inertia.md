## 1. Global sampling density
Let Gamma be the set of distinct complex ordinates z=(rho-1/2)/i, m_z their finite positive multiplicities. Let K=ell^2(Gamma,m_z), with inner product linear in its first argument. Define J on K by (Jy)_z=y_(conj z). Multiplicity symmetry makes J a self-adjoint unitary. For a compact smooth function f on R let V f=(F[f](z))_z, F[f](z)=int f(x)e^(izx)dx. Integration by parts on the bounded imaginary strip and the classical zero count make Vf a vector of K. GSG01's unconditional formula reads q(f,g)=<Vf,J Vg>.

Claim: V(C_c^infty(R)) is dense in K. If y in K is orthogonal to this linear subspace, take a fixed compact smooth f0 whose Fourier transform is nonzero at every ordinate. Such f0 exists by GSG01 countable dilation avoidance: start with integral-one bump; each analytic transform at rz has only discrete zeros in r, and choose r outside the countable union. Every translate T_t f0 is again a test. Orthogonality gives
    0 = sum_z c_z e^(itz), c_z=m_z F[f0](z) conj(y_z).
Cauchy-Schwarz gives sum |c_z|<=||Vf0||_K ||y||_K. The sum is uniformly absolutely convergent on compact real t intervals since |Im z|<=1/2. Integrating over t>=0 against e^(-st), Re s>1/2, gives 0=sum c_z/(s-iz). By the normal-convergence argument in GSG01 this is a meromorphic function on C with residues c_z at the distinct locally finite pole locations iz. Identity theorem forces all c_z=0. Hence y=0. This is a density proof in the positive sampling norm even when q itself is indefinite; it is not an assertion of density for any fixed support window.

## 2. Exact limiting negative index
The negative subspace K_- of J is the closed span of the vectors e_z-e_(conj z) for one representative of each nonreal conjugate pair, normalized in K. Real ordinates lie in K_+. Its dimension r is the number of distinct nonreal pairs, possibly infinite; multiplicities merely change the normalization and not r.

For any window (-a,a), any q-negative definite subspace of tests injects via P_- V into K_-: a vector with vanishing negative component has q>=0. Thus its dimension is at most r. To extend to the closed form domain, use the compact smooth form core and approximate any finite collection of negative spectral eigenvectors simultaneously in the form norm. Their Gram and q matrices converge, preserving independence and strict negative definiteness. The compact-resolvent minmax principle therefore gives n_-(A_a)<=r (nontrivial when r is finite).

Conversely fix any k<=r finite and orthonormal y_1,...,y_k in K_-. By section 1 choose tests f_j with ||Vf_j-y_j||_K<delta, where sqrt(k)delta<1/4. For c in C^k let Yc=sum c_j y_j, Xc=sum c_j Vf_j. Then ||X-Y||<=sqrt(k)delta=e<1/4, and ||J||=1 implies
    q(sum c_j f_j) = <Xc,J Xc>
        <= (-1+2e+e^2)||c||^2 < 0 for c!=0.
In particular the tests are independent. All supports fit inside some common finite (-a_k,a_k), so minmax gives n_-(A_a)>=k for every a>=a_k. Therefore
    lim_(a->infinity) n_-(A_a) = r
in the extended nonnegative integers. For finite r it is exactly r for all sufficiently large a, not merely a limit of finite truncation matrices. For infinite r it tends to infinity. One pair counts once even at a multiple zero. The factor of two in the count of distinct off-line zeta zeros follows from conjugate-ordinate symmetry; for zeta a usual quartet contributes two negative squares. No quantitative bound on a_k from density. No implication that every fixed window sees every pair. RH remains open.

Optional parity refinement: reflection (Rf)(x)=f(-x) corresponds to (Py)_z=y_(-z), a self-adjoint unitary commuting with J. Even and odd tests are dense in the respective P eigenspaces by symmetrizing the global dense range. Repeat the proof on those sectors: their limiting negative indices are dimensions of K_- intersect K_even and K_odd. A four-element orbit {z,conj z,-z,-conj z} contributes one to each; a purely imaginary pair contributes zero even and one odd. This agrees with the pattern in Bombieri Theorem 9 and does not require claiming absence of imaginary pairs in this abstract model.
