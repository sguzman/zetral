## Audit

PPE01 uses the exact support-constrained scalar Fourier form, not an unrestricted multiplication representation of A_a. Splitting at t=0 when the carrier is positive makes the reflected Fourier factor always a distance at least omega from its carrier on one half-line. Cauchy-Schwarz with the fourth moment bounds the bounded arithmetic cross term by O(exp(A)/(1+h omega)^2) and the log-symbol cross term by O_h((1+h omega)^-1). Parity symmetrization produces actual orthogonal ordinary trial functions with negligible reflected cross energy on the FES02 anomalous packet sequence. This alone cannot transfer arbitrary index counts.

PPE02 bypasses that limitation. The derivative of the proposed principal-value Fourier transform is exactly h F[A](hz)/pi and its value at zero is zero; this gives the real odd entire sign approximation with compact distribution support. Plancherel gives the mass-one normalization of k and the real-axis contraction. The multiplier is nonzero away from a null set, and its ordinary and log-norm contractions give injective support-enlarging form-domain transfer. Real-entire symmetry is crucial: the paired product uses m(z)^2, NOT |m(z)|^2 at a nonreal node. Uniform strip deficit decay and the classical local count yield an absolutely convergent bounded difference form; the point-evaluation bound I_a(B) controls both factors without an assumption on the input's carrier or derivative. At B>0 it is at most exp(2Ba)/(2B). The sign filter flips parity, and contraction turns the original negative subspace into a genuine subspace of the same dimension at the enlarged window. The count inequalities imply pairing of anomalous limsup and liminf exponents; the support shift is retained. There is no same-window relative prefactor claim.

PPE03's base sinc-fourth density is a compact transform and gives a real sign deficit with a polynomial lower bound as well as an upper bound. The lower bound follows by integrating the periodic positive mean of sin^4 over [t,2t]. This is needed: a rapid-decay base with no explicit lower margin would not justify preserving contraction after interpolation. The cardinal polynomial times a small-support smooth transform is exactly one at its own node and zero at the other finite nodes. The exponential exp[-i sign(Im z_j)L(z-z_j)] has real-axis modulus exp(-L|Im z_j|), while it is one at its target. Reality and oddness symmetrizations preserve the interpolation data, and the weighted real-axis sup norm can be made smaller than half the base margin. The correction has finite support radius, not a uniform arbitrarily small radius. The final multiplier is injective by analyticity and its nonzero real limits, and its complex deficit remains summable against the zero count. At all selected fast nodes the squared multiplier is exactly one; the real-zero difference is nonpositive and the other nonreal terms have exponent at most 2b. Thus no dimension loss is needed. If b=0 and all nonreal nodes were selected, the upper comparison has exactly zero error, even though the enlargement may be large. The threshold for anomalous rate pairing is consequently 2B_tail rather than 2B. It remains compatible with distinct finite-zero parity prefactors at a common fixed window.

## Next question: separate finite fast modes from the residual ordinary spectrum

The interpolation technique can be adapted to a real even contraction tending to one on the real axis, with exact zeros at a finite reflection-invariant fast set. Start with the square of the sinc-based sign approximation, whose distance to the unit bound still has polynomial lower margin; add a small real-even cardinal correction. It should map every residual-form negative trial space into the common fast-evaluation kernel while preserving its depth up to an error at the remaining zero-deviation scale. This is different from the prior fast-difference constraints, which were not preserved by frequency filtering.

A second observation suggests a finite-dimensional inertia reduction. For fixed finitely many fast nodes, the normalized ordinary evaluation columns have a positive endpoint Cauchy Gram limit. Choose fixed compact smooth endpoint profiles forming a dual frame to those normalized evaluations, with uniformly bounded right inverse for large windows. The residual paired form acting from these smooth profiles to arbitrary ordinary L2 vectors is bounded by the classical local count and rapid decay of the smooth profile evaluations: expect O(exp(2ba)) for remaining deviations <=b>0. Then split the whole form domain into the smooth finite-dimensional right inverse and the common fast-evaluation kernel. At a depth threshold H strictly between the fast and remaining exponential scales, the fast finite block of q+H||.||^2 has known inertia and a small inverse. Its Schur correction on the residual kernel should be o(H) in ordinary operator norm, even if that residual contains anomalous negative modes.

Combine this with the annihilating filter to seek an exact additive decomposition of ordinary indexed growth rates above the tail deviation threshold: finite genuine zero scales plus any residual anomaly spectrum. This would localize the unresolved ordinary-rate problem to the infinite tail without assuming bounded residual density. These are proposed questions, not claims already proved here.
