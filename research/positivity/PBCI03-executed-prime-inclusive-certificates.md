## Mathematical and arithmetic protocol

Use PBCI01–02 with a single physical cell of length h. Low parity modes are the first one, two or four Neumann indices in that parity. Retain J=256 positive boundary rate terms in the lower operator, and the first S=24 of those terms in its high comparison; include the positive even pole there as another column. The odd pole loss on high modes is enclosed by the n^-4 coefficient sum using all n greater than the last low index plus one (the code uses the parity gap: for highest odd index 1,3,7, sum from n>=3,5,9 bounded by 1/(3*2³), 1/(3*4³), 1/(3*8³)). At prime-inclusive windows the prime norm equals b=log 2/sqrt 2 by disjoint strip exchange; use it in the high diagonal comparison. The low and cross prime entries use the exact Wronskian and diagonal formulas of PBCI02, with no prime contributions omitted. All tested h<=0.9<log 3, so only prime 2 can contribute.

In implementation the normalized overlaps are multiplied by sqrt(h), namely v_n(r)=nu_n r/(r²+omega_n²), nu_0=1, nu_n=sqrt2 otherwise. The positive rate coefficient is d_r=2(1-eta exp(-rh))/h, and the pole coefficient is 2 eta/h with corresponding overlap nu_n sinh(h/4)/(omega_n²+1/4) in even parity or -nu_n cosh(h/4)/(omega_n²+1/4) in odd parity. This retains the normalization without computing sqrt(h). For nonconstant low indices the exact alpha value is replaced by alpha0+2 sum_(j<J) omega²/[r_j(r_j²+omega²)], a LOWER diagonal value since the omitted digamma terms are positive. It is not an exact full low block. Cross entries are unaffected because D is diagonal. On the high block use the lower weights w_n from DNB01. Thus the tested finite matrix is still a lower comparison.

Floating arithmetic chooses trial coefficients by 80 coordinate sweeps of the projected variational cost; every coefficient is rounded to an exact rational with denominator 10^12. This is NOT a trusted numerical inverse: the inequality holds for arbitrary trial coefficients. Recompute the cost and all remaining terms by outward-rounded BigInt arithmetic on scale 10^18. Exp, log, pi and Euler gamma bounds inherit the elementary finite series of MBL03; sqrt2 is checked by integer squaring. Trigonometric arguments of actual prime shifts are reduced by an integer multiple of 2pi and enclosed by 40 Taylor terms and a symmetric next-term error, since the reduced arguments have magnitude at most 2pi plus tiny enclosure error. No zeta ordinates are supplied.

The combined high tail coefficient includes the subtraction of the selected trial columns before bounding the finite-rate and pole n^-2 contributions. For one/two-mode tests use the PBCI02 prime n^-1 bound with M=256. Four-mode tests use M=512 and a sharper parity estimate: for n>M and k<=K<M,
    |P_nk| <= (4b/pi) [M/(M-k)]/n,
because 2 b k_n k_k/(omega_n-omega_k)<=4b/[pi(n-k)]. For each parity sum n^-2 over n>M is <=1/[2(M-1)] by integrating the decreasing function on intervals of length two before each included n. Thus the prime weighted squared tail is <= (4b M/[pi(M-k)])²/[2(M-1)g_(M+1)]. Use (a+b)²<=2(a²+b²) for combined prime and smooth tail (no factor 2 for prime-free). Sum column tail squares to bound the high residual Gram by a scalar identity.

The scalar tests certify a positive lower Schur margin; the two-mode tests certify positive first diagonal and determinant. The first four-mode test uses interval LDL directly; the refined version subtracts the exact outward tail upper bound times identity, replaces each symmetric matrix entry interval by an exact fixed-point midpoint plus radius, and subtracts the maximum radius row sum times identity. The resulting exact rational lower matrix is tested by outward-rounded LDL pivots. Positive lower pivots and positive high diagonal weights imply strict positivity on the entire fixed window, not just the low Ritz space. Pivots/determinants are NOT lowest eigenvalues. Failure is inconclusive for the actual operator.
