## 3. Disposition: signed zero-measure cancellation

The density method bounds only the negative squares, deliberately discarding the positive squares. Hence a large population of off-line zeros is not sufficient for an anomalous negative mode. A distinct productive question is to retain the cancellation by considering high-carrier local complex zero measures normalized by log of the carrier.

The arithmetic explicit formula on any FIXED compact smooth modulation envelope gives normalized q(M_omega f,M_omega g)-><f,g> as |omega|->infinity: the log archimedean shift supplies the identity, and the bounded finite-window arithmetic part divided by log|omega| tends to zero. By the classical local count one can extract subsequential positive locally finite measures on the horizontal relative-coordinate strip; paired zero form convergence against fixed analytic tests should impose an exact analytic projection identity. An appropriate positive strip Poisson kernel could identify that projection as uniform Lebesgue measure on the two strip boundaries. This may allow cancellation estimates from signed discrepancy rather than absolute off-line population. It also suggests countermodels: a horizontally translation-invariant off-line population has the same paired analytic projection as critical-line population, hence can be invisible to this fixed-envelope normalization even with weighted density exponent one.

Next: prove the local measure limit and projection identity, classify at least the translation-invariant examples and nontrivial nonuniform admissible examples, then quantify what stronger moving-window discrepancy is necessary for anomalous growth. Do not infer a global spectral bound from fixed-test convergence. This is a distinct route, not a claim already established here.
