# Campaign LXXXVI — The critical limit of shifted-xi canonical systems

Date opened: 2026-11-27. Status: CLOSED WITHIN SCOPE (2026-11-27).

## Precise question

Can the de Branges spaces and self-adjoint canonical-system extensions
generated by

    E^omega(z) = xi(1/2 + omega - i z),  omega > 0,

be placed on a common Hilbert-space scale so that an identified operator,
resolvent, or spectral measure has a nontrivial limit as `omega -> 0+`, whose
spectrum corresponds to the critical-line zeta ordinates under RH? Can that
limit also be related to Zetral's arithmetic Weil form by an exact compression
identity?

## Existing evidence / prior art

Suzuki, *A canonical system of differential equations arising from the
Riemann zeta-function*, RIMS Kôkyûroku Bessatsu B34 (2012), 397–435,
Proposition 1.2 and Theorem 2.3, constructs the shifted-xi route. The
Hermite–Biehler condition is equivalent to the corresponding quotient
`Theta_omega` being meromorphic inner; it holds for `omega>=1/2`
unconditionally and for `0<omega<1/2` under RH (sufficient condition). The
canonical system is explicitly constructed in that paper for `omega>1`.
The self-adjoint extensions have spectra at zeros of the shifted functions
`A^omega,B^omega`, not directly at zeros of xi. At `omega=0`, `E^0` is
real-entire and the strict Hermite–Biehler inequality collapses to equality.
See the source audit `research/operators/B3-07-shifted-xi-canonical-system-endpoint.md`.

The positivity program has distinct arithmetic local forms and a compression
identity in the Suzuki model, but no established identification between those
forms and the shifted-xi canonical-system Hilbert spaces. Prior art includes
the existing candidate construction, so the investigation is about endpoint
operator convergence and arithmetic linkage, not claiming a new canonical
system.

## Design and falsification

1. Pin and inspect Theorem 2.3's Hamiltonian/determinant construction and its
   exact parameter restrictions; distinguish what is established for
   `omega>1`, `omega>=1/2`, and RH-conditional smaller shifts.
2. Test natural common-space identifications (model-space quotient,
   de Branges boundary norm, or spectral measure). Derive candidate resolvent
   or kernel limits on compact spectral sets as `omega -> 0+`; look for norm
   blowup, loss of strict positivity, or nonunique renormalization.
3. Independently audit whether zeros of `A^omega` converge with multiplicity
   to xi zeros under RH; separate local uniform entire-function convergence
   from convergence of self-adjoint operators.
4. Require a concrete unitary/embedding map from the limiting operator model
   to Zetral's Weil completion and prove the inverse-compression identity,
   including domains and multiplicities. A formal match of zero sets is not
   enough.
5. If endpoint degeneration precludes a canonical limit, prove the specific
   obstruction and preserve possible renormalized alternatives.

## Persistent backlog entry

- **Hypothesis:** Although direct substitution at `omega=0` is degenerate,
  suitably normalized positive-shift resolvents or spectral measures may
  converge to a nontrivial operator encoding the critical xi zeros.
- **Prerequisites:** B3-07; Suzuki 2012 Proposition 1.2, Theorem 2.3, §§1.1–1.2;
  canonical-system inverse spectral theory; exact RH/multiplicity assumptions;
  Zetral's arithmetic compression theorem.
- **Information gain:** A valid limit would give a genuine operator route
  beyond finite positivity; a precise divergence/degeneration theorem would
  eliminate this interpolation/limit strategy.
- **Dependencies:** All common-space maps and renormalizations must be
  explicitly defined. Spectral correspondence and arithmetic compression are
  separate obligations.
- **Acceptance:** Prove a nontrivial operator or spectral-measure convergence
  with stated topology, domains, and spectral correspondence plus an
  arithmetic bridge; or establish a precise obstruction to canonical endpoint
  convergence. Mere local uniform convergence of entire functions does not
  pass.
- **Verification:** Independent source and operator-domain audit. No costly
  local computation; use Nix for any future reproducible computation.

## Checkpoint B3-07

Direct substitution is impossible in the strict de Branges construction:
`E^0=E^0#`, so the Hermite–Biehler inequality is equality and
`Theta_0=1`. This proves only degeneration of the naive endpoint, not
impossibility of a renormalized limit. The positive-shift spectral family is
established prior art; the common-space/operator limit and arithmetic identity
remain unproved.

## Checkpoint B3-08: unscaled model spaces vanish, first-order kernel survives

Under RH, `Theta_omega -> 1` locally uniformly on `C+`. In the standard
common ambient Hardy space, the model-space projections therefore converge
strongly to zero, as verified directly from the diagonal model kernel and
density of Hardy kernels. However, `k_omega/omega` has a locally uniform,
positive-semidefinite limit expressed through `xi'/xi` in `Re(s)>1/2`.
This gives a concrete renormalized RKHS candidate but no operator convergence
or arithmetic compression. Full proof and scope are in
`research/operators/B3-08-model-space-collapse-and-rescaled-kernel.md`.
Next determine whether this first-order kernel has an independently known
Nevanlinna/de Branges realization and whether any candidate multiplication
operator has the needed zeta spectral measure.

## Checkpoint B3-09: exact zero-measure realization of the limit kernel

Under RH, the even Hadamard product of `xi(1/2+w)` gives a paired logarithmic
derivative expansion. Substitution into the B3-08 kernel identifies it
exactly as the Herglotz/Cauchy-transform kernel of the signed discrete
zero-counting measure, with analytic multiplicity as atom weight. Its RKHS is
unitarily the Cauchy-transform image of `L^2(mu)`, and multiplication by the
real coordinate is self-adjoint with spectrum the distinct signed ordinates.
The eigenspaces remain one-dimensional; multiplicity is spectral-measure
weight, not geometric dimension. This remains RH-conditional and zero-defined.
See `research/operators/B3-09-nevanlinna-kernel-and-zero-measure.md`.

Next compare the explicit limit kernel and multiplication operator with the
arithmetic Weil local forms: derive the candidate compression map, test its
norm on compact-test vectors, and search for a falsifiable mismatch before
claiming any operator correspondence.

## Checkpoint B3-10: exact arithmetic transport, but no operator-limit proof

The Cauchy-transform RKHS in B3-09 is unitarily identified with Suzuki's
global Weil completion by composing its Cauchy transform with the source's
weighted zero-sampling map. On compact tests the map is the explicit series
`(1/sqrt(pi)) sum_gamma m_gamma hat(u)(gamma)/(gamma-z)`, and its norm is
exactly the Weil form. Unitary conjugation transports the global
self-adjoint operator and Theorem 3.3 inverse-compression identity with all
domains intact. This is an exact bridge for the *endpoint model*, conditional
on RH and Suzuki's source theorems; it is not independent of zero data and
does not prove strong-resolvent convergence from positive shifts. Full audit:
`research/operators/B3-10-arithmetic-compression-transport.md`.

Next audit the family of self-adjoint extensions whose eigenvalues are zeros
of `A^omega`: establish vague convergence of their eigenvalue-counting
measures by Hurwitz, then test whether any common-space operator convergence
can preserve multiplicity. Keep counting-measure convergence separate from
geometric eigenspace multiplicity.

## Checkpoint B3-11: positive-shift eigenvalue counting measures converge

Under RH, the associated self-adjoint extensions have simple real spectra
given by the zeros of `A^omega` for every positive shift. Since
`A^omega -> xi(1/2-i z)` locally uniformly, Hurwitz's theorem proves vague
convergence of their eigenvalue-counting measures to the xi zero-divisor
measure, including multiplicities as cluster masses. This is the same measure
as B3-09's Herglotz measure. It does not prove strong-resolvent convergence:
when a limiting zero is multiple, the scalar endpoint RKHS still has only one
eigendirection at that ordinate. Details and exact source hypotheses are in
`research/operators/B3-11-shifted-spectrum-vague-limit.md`.

Next determine whether a common-space identification can promote this vague
measure limit to strong-resolvent convergence, or whether a multiplicity
fiber obstruction prevents that for the scalar RKHS limit. The zero-divisor
measure convergence itself is accepted only as a conditional spectral-measure
result, not a complete operator limit.
