## Findings

No discrepancy found in the checked argument.

1. **Maximal-set synthesis.** For `alpha_u=B+iu`, the half-line profiles have squared norm `1/[2B|1+alpha_u|^2]`. The local zero-count upper bound makes the multiplicity-weighted sum finite, so the synthesis `E` is Hilbert–Schmidt. The injectivity proof is sound: with `b_u=sqrt(m_u)c_u/(1-alpha_u^2)`, Cauchy–Schwarz gives `sum |b_u|<infinity`; the identity `sum b_u exp(-iu s)=exp(-(1-B)s)sum b_u` then isolates each coefficient by Cesàro averaging after multiplication by `exp(iv s)`. The decaying right side has zero average, while absolute summability justifies termwise averaging on the left. This proves dense range of `E*`, not a bounded inverse, as the note correctly limits the conclusion.

2. **Sign counts and phase stability.** The signed-coordinate involution `J_theta` has one positive and one negative direction for each pair `±u`. Dense range of `E*` transfers every finite-dimensional strictly signed subspace to `E J_theta E*`; in the finite case projection onto the corresponding signed coordinate subspace gives the matching upper bound. For the compact phase product, trace-norm continuity follows by first truncating the uniformly summable rank-one series and then using coordinatewise continuity on the finite remainder. Thus each fixed positive/negative eigenvalue magnitude is continuous. Since injectivity gives the sign count at every phase, compactness yields a positive minimum for each fixed index. This is set- and index-dependent, not a uniform bound over changing zero sets or all indices.

3. **Trace oscillation.** Pairing `u` and `-u` makes `T_B(a)` self-adjoint. The diagonal rank-one trace is `1/[2 alpha_u(1+alpha_u)^2]`; the coefficient series is absolutely summable by the same local count. Its coefficient at each nonzero frequency `2u` is nonzero. Cesàro averaging against that frequency rules out convergence of the trace to a constant. The uniform trace-norm tail control in SES02 is enough to pass from convergence of every fixed parity-resolved signed depth to convergence of their sums, so the stated conclusion that at least one such prefactor fails to converge follows. It does not imply that every selected depth oscillates.

4. **Indexed exponential growth.** For `b>b_j`, the definition of the finite-subset order statistic implies at most `j-1` pairs have deviation greater than `b`. Imposing one linear constraint per such pair makes their paired-form contribution nonnegative; every `j`-dimensional space intersects that constraint kernel, giving the stated upper rate from the remaining trace-norm bound. The lower rate follows from NIR01's exact-cardinal finite-band tests: their outside-band sample error decays geometrically, while convolution by the normalized modulated box filter is contractive in both `L2` and the first-derivative norm. The test synthesis norm is therefore `O(L^{-n})`; taking `n=floor((a-h0)/h)` and then `h` large yields `2 min_{S} d`, and the supremum over `j`-element sets is `2b_j`. This is a theoretical existence argument dependent on exact finite-band zero data and inherited NIR01 estimates, not a practical or zero-independent algorithm.

5. **Infinite-set interpolation and faster finite scales.** The Hardy-space cardinal construction is consistent with the H1 metric because `U f=(1-iz)Ff` is the isometry onto upper-half-plane `H2`; `B_j(z)/(z+i)` lies in `H2`, and its preimage has the claimed zero/nonzero evaluations. The Blaschke product convergence condition is met by `sum d_l/(1+u_l^2)<infinity`. For the subordinate-scale argument, cardinal vectors against the union of faster and current nodes are orthogonal to the faster evaluation span and isolate the current coordinates. The fast finite block has diverging absolute eigenvalues after scaling, so its Schur correction on bounded spectral thresholds is `o(1)`; the note correctly limits this argument to finitely many faster pairs and fixed-index thresholds.
