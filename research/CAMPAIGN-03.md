## Bounded research questions

- D01: sharpen the exact fixed-index upper bound by exploiting the counting distribution of the sampling frequencies instead of bounding every frequency solely by reciprocal-square summability. Check the power of X and multiplicities, record proof and prior-art status.
- D02: derive a two-sided spectral comparison reducing lower-bound/sharpness questions to approximation defects of the leading invariant subspaces. Identify what follows from abstract variational theory and what requires new arithmetic or analytic input.
- D03: pursue a concrete lower-bound or sharpness mechanism in the actual weighted sampling spaces, e.g. dual annihilators of compact-support transforms; establish a defensible lower bound or document the obstruction. Do not mistake a trial-vector upper estimate for an optimality result. Compare related literature and test counterexamples.
- D04: consolidate the strongest supported results, limitations, proofs and source comparison; assess whether to continue sharpness, pivot to a better mathematical question, or prioritize verification. Create the next bounded campaign if the project remains scientifically productive.

## Acceptance and autonomy

Each milestone is dispositioned with durable proof, reproducible evidence or a specific negative result/blocker. A matching lower bound or novel theorem is not guaranteed. No indefinite effort on one dead end, no manufactured milestones, no unnecessary repetition of infrastructure work. On closure choose the next productive campaign and persist the rationale; no routine owner approval needed for in-scope mathematical research. External submission, new expenses, access changes and changes to the overarching mission require explicit owner direction. Work is performed during conversation turns, not autonomously in the background.
