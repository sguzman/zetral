Research question

H ψₙ = γₙ ψₙ

Can arithmetic define a meaningful spectral system whose eigenvalues encode the nontrivial zeta zeros? Our first campaign investigates the quantitative convergence of spectral approximations constructed using Weil's quadratic form.

Candidate result

0 < ta,n − γn ≤ Kn X3/2 log X e−2πX

For fixed n and sufficiently large a, with X = e2a, under RH and Suzuki's sampling and inverse-compression results. The argument combines a zero-isolating theta kernel, compact truncation and a quadratic Ritz estimate. Five supporting scalar lemmas passed Lean verification; the full analytic and spectral proof remains unformalized and independently unreviewed. Campaign III refines the earlier upper bound and gives a positive finite-parameter lower bound; matching asymptotic sharpness is unresolved. Manuscript v0.2 gives explicit constants for the earlier, weaker upper estimate.

Small-window asymptotics

a ta,n = (n − ½)π + dn/ℓ + qn/ℓ² + on(ℓ⁻²)

For fixed n as a decreases to zero, ℓ = log(1/a). The first correction dn = Si((2n−1)π)/2 is strictly positive; the second has an explicit convergent integral. The local candidate derivation does not assume RH, unlike the global large-window comparison. The proof uses Suzuki's unconditional local extension theorem and direct prime-free coercivity. The second-order coefficient evaluations and worker finite spectra are non-interval-certified diagnostics; independent human review and priority remain outstanding.
